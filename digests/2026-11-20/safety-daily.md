# Daily Digest (2026-11-20)

## Top 3 Developments

**CSET Warns Military AI Decision-Making Creates a New Data-Integrity Attack Surface** - [The Data Is the Target](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/the-data-is-the-target/) (Georgetown CSET, via Lawfare) argues that as militaries lean on AI for decision support, adversaries gain incentive to manipulate underlying data rather than attack models directly. This matters because it identifies an attack surface current defense-AI governance frameworks largely overlook.

**Frontier Models Can Unmask Authorship of Text From a Single Prompt** - A new [study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26216v1) finds Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol, and Gemini 4 Carbon can determine from one zero-shot prompt whether text is AI- or human-written, identify the generating lab, and sometimes name the human author, matching trained detectors like Pangram. This matters because the same capability that aids provenance and misinformation detection could also be used to deanonymize writers at scale.

**Post-Training Found to Widen the Gap Between What Models Represent and What They Report** - [Represented but misread](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26584v1) shows across six open-weight base/post-trained model pairs that RLHF-style post-training can degrade a model's ability to correctly read out information it still internally represents, sometimes inverting judgments that linear probes recover cleanly. This matters because it directly undermines confidence in model self-reports as a safety-oversight signal, reinforcing a theme running through this week's eval-tells and confabulation findings.

## Risk Watch

**Increased Risks**
- Frontier models can unmask AI-vs-human authorship and sometimes name authors from a single prompt, creating a dual-use deanonymization risk ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26216v1)).
- Post-training widens the gap between what models internally represent and what they report, undermining trust in self-report-based oversight ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26584v1)).
- Corporate AI-incident disclosures are shaped by investor materiality, systematically underreporting harms that fall on the public ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25841v1)).
- Multimodal safety-guardrail rationales are often hallucinated even when classifications are correct, creating false assurance in auditing ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25793v1)).
- Military reliance on AI decision-making creates a new data-manipulation attack surface that current governance overlooks ([CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/the-data-is-the-target/)).
