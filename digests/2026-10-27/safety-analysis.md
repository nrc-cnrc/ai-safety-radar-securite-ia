# Blogs & News (2026-10-27)

## Top Stories

- **[Measuring agent capabilities on open-ended AI R&D tasks](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cTiEPgkvDRxTFwtyu/measuring-agent-capabilities-on-open-ended-ai-r-and-d-tasks-1)** — New empirical work tracks frontier model performance on four long-horizon, open-ended AI R&D tasks, finding an overall capability doubling time of roughly one year since 2024. Notably, models improve faster on engineering-heavy tasks (7-month doubling) than on more conceptual research tasks (16.8-month doubling), suggesting a persistent gap between automatable "grunt work" and genuine research insight. This matters because it offers one of the more rigorous empirical anchors for forecasting when AI systems could meaningfully accelerate (or bottleneck) AI research itself — a key input for takeoff-speed and recursive self-improvement debates.

- **[Anthropic files S-1 with the SEC for proposed initial public offering](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec)** — Anthropic has formally moved toward going public, a major milestone that will subject the company to new disclosure requirements and shareholder pressures. This matters because public-market incentives (quarterly earnings, investor expectations) could create tension with Anthropic's safety-first mission, and the S-1 filing itself may become a rich source of previously-undisclosed information about the company's finances, governance structure, and risk disclosures relevant to AI safety commitments.

- **[How Black-Box Distillation Works](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-black-box-distillation-works/)** (Georgetown CSET) — This technical explainer breaks down how adversarial distillation from proprietary U.S. models likely occurs in practice, informing live legislative debates about restricting such techniques. It highlights significant uncertainty about exactly which methods Chinese labs used and how much distillation has actually accelerated China's frontier AI progress. This matters because policy proposals to restrict distillation are moving forward with limited technical grounding, and CSET's analysis is a rare attempt to ground that debate in how the underlying mechanics actually work.

## Policy & Governance

- **[How Black-Box Distillation Works](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-black-box-distillation-works/)** (Georgetown CSET) — See above; directly informs proposed U.S. legislation targeting model distillation, making it essential reading for policymakers weighing export-control-style restrictions on model outputs and APIs.

- **[What Should the Rules for A.I. Companies Actually Be? We Asked the Experts.](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/what-should-the-rules-for-a-i-companies-actually-be-we-asked-the-experts)** (AI Now Institute) — In this roundtable, AI Now's Kate Brennan argues for shifting AI governance discourse away from speculative abstract risk framing and toward concrete, present-day harms from corporate AI deployment (labor, economic, and safety harms). This matters because it represents a notable strand of the policy community pushing back against existential-risk-centric framing in favor of accountability for current corporate practices, a divide increasingly shaping how regulation gets designed and prioritized.
