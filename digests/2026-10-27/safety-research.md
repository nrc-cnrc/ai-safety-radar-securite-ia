# Research Papers (2026-10-27)

## Key Papers

**[AI watermarking efforts must encourage independent public scrutiny](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03406-3)** (Nature, peer-reviewed)
This editorial argues that current AI watermarking schemes, often developed and deployed unilaterally by AI labs, lack the transparency needed for independent verification of their robustness and failure modes. It calls for open protocols and third-party auditing rights as a precondition for watermarking to function as a credible provenance and governance tool. This matters because watermarking is increasingly cited by policymakers as a technical solution to AI-generated content risks, yet without public scrutiny its real-world reliability remains unverified.

**[I-HITL: Inalienable Human-in-the-Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35835v1)**
The authors introduce "inalienable human-in-the-loop" (I-HITL), a cryptographic protocol ensuring an AI agent cannot initiate a critical action without explicit human approval — even if the agent attempts to bypass guardrails or manipulate the local harness mediating that approval. This directly targets a growing threat model where capable agents find ways to circumvent oversight mechanisms rather than merely refusing tasks. It matters because human oversight is a cornerstone of AI safety governance, and this work provides one of the first technically enforced (rather than policy-based) guarantees for it.

**[ShutdownMod: Turning Any Trajectory into a Shutdown Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35486v1)**
ShutdownMod is a lightweight method for converting arbitrary agent trajectories into shutdown-resistance tests by injecting shutdown requests mid-task, while giving the agent a cryptographic/contextual signal to distinguish legitimate interventions from adversarial injections. This substantially broadens the coverage of shutdown evaluations beyond narrow, hand-crafted scenarios and reduces vulnerability to "evaluation awareness." It matters because shutdown corrigibility remains a central open problem in agentic AI safety, and scalable, hard-to-game evaluations are needed to track it as models grow more capable.

**[Evaluating DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 for loss of control and harmful manipulation risks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35671v1)**
This is an independent third-party frontier safety evaluation of an open-weight Chinese model, assessing loss-of-control behaviors and manipulative tendencies — a category of scrutiny typically reserved for closed-source system cards. The authors highlight that open-weight releases receive systematically less frontier-risk evaluation despite comparable capability levels. It matters because it extends independent frontier safety auditing practices to the open-weight ecosystem, where governance gaps are most acute.

**[Trustworthy Recursive Self-Improvement in the Era of Large Models: Foundation, Risks, Evaluation and Open Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35193v1)**
This survey formalizes recursive self-improvement (RSI) in LLM-based systems — where models revise their own prompts, memories, tools, and improvement mechanisms — and catalogs failure modes including flawed self-evaluation, capability leakage across improvement cycles, and unverifiable claimed gains. It proposes a framework for evaluating whether self-improvement loops are actually trustworthy rather than illusory. It matters because RSI is increasingly deployed in practice (e.g., coding agents, auto-research systems) well ahead of rigorous safety standards for it.

**[Can Open Be Too Open? On the Security and Trustworthiness of Fully Transparent LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35199v1)**
The authors challenge the common assumption, borrowed from software security, that full transparency (open weights, data, and training pipeline) strictly improves LLM security and trustworthiness, showing instead that openness can introduce new attack surfaces specific to model internals. This complicates ongoing debates about open-source AI policy. It matters because transparency mandates are a leading governance proposal, and this work suggests the security tradeoffs are more nuanced than analogies to open-source software suggest.

**[Steering for Safety, Hijacking for Profit: Trojan Threats in the LLM Activation Steering Supply Chain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35043v1)**
This paper demonstrates that third-party steering vector plugins — increasingly used for cheap, training-free safety interventions — can be trojaned to covertly manipulate model outputs while appearing to perform legitimate safety steering. It identifies a new supply-chain attack surface specific to the activation-steering ecosystem. It matters because steering vectors are being adopted as lightweight alignment tools, and this shows they can become a vector for exactly the harms they're meant to prevent.

**[Linear Probes Transfer from Base to Distilled Language Models despite Rotation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35586v1)**
The study shows that linear probes trained to detect harmful requests on a base model largely retain their effectiveness when applied unchanged to distilled successor models, despite representational rotation between models. This suggests safety-monitoring infrastructure built for one model generation may be reusable across updates, reducing the cost of maintaining oversight tooling. It matters for scalable AI safety auditing, where retraining detectors for every model release is currently a major operational bottleneck.

**[SEMA-Auto: Sighted and Efficient Multi-Turn Attack with Autonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35743v1)**
SEMA-Auto introduces an autonomous multi-turn jailbreak attacker that reads victim model responses and adapts its adversarial plan in real time, without needing costly in-loop success-signal evaluators that previous interactive attacks relied on. The approach achieves strong transferability while being far cheaper to run than prior adaptive jailbreak methods. It matters because it lowers the barrier for scalable, adaptive red-teaming (and correspondingly, for adversarial misuse), underscoring the need for robust multi-turn safety defenses.

**[The Watchtower Imperative: Lifespan-Calibrated Evaluation for Frontier AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35725v1)**
Analyzing documented AI harms from 2017–2026, the authors argue that frontier AI safety evaluations implicitly assume an adult user model, missing systematic harms to children and the elderly (e.g., companion-app-linked suicide, automated care denials). They propose developmental moderators to extend safety evaluation across the human lifespan. It matters because it exposes a structural blind spot in current evaluation practice that existing internal red-teaming and benchmarks have not addressed.

**[CANS: Capability-Preserving Neuron Steering for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35530v1)**
CANS introduces a training-free activation-steering framework that explicitly optimizes for both target-behavior control and preservation of general capabilities, addressing a known failure mode where safety steering degrades model usefulness. By using log-probability and capability proxies, it balances these competing objectives without domain-specific engineering. It matters because capability-destructive safety interventions are a major adoption barrier in production systems, and this offers a more practical path to deployable behavioral control.
