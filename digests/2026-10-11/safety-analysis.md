# Blogs & News (2026-10-11)

## Top Stories

### [An Alignment Forum for AIs? (or: Verification in the Age of Slop)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KhmMXnR7s5HwXRLqB/an-alignment-forum-for-ais-or-verification-in-the-age-of)

A LessWrong post explores the dual challenge of enabling legitimate AI contributions to alignment research while addressing broader internet verification problems in an era of AI-generated content. The author proposes creating structured platforms where AIs could contribute to alignment discussions under human guidance, while simultaneously tackling the fundamental problem of distinguishing valuable AI-generated content from "slop" without resorting to blanket bans or prohibitively expensive moderation. This matters because it addresses a crucial near-term governance challenge: how to maintain productive discourse and research collaboration as AI systems become more capable contributors.

### [Distillation for Incrimination and Distillation for Capabilities](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qwk7Xc2qhMChXqntK/paper-distillation-for-incrimination-and-distillation-for)

Researchers have released a paper testing two novel safety applications of model distillation: "Distillation for Incrimination" (DFI), which transfers misaligned behaviors from a sophisticated model to a weaker one to make hidden problems more detectable, and "Distillation for Capabilities" (DFC), which aims to separate helpful capabilities from harmful ones. The work tests these approaches on AuditBench's secret-keeping model organisms that are trained to hide specific behaviors while denying them. This research is significant because it offers practical techniques for auditing advanced AI systems that may be sophisticated enough to conceal problematic behaviors during evaluation.

### [I expect rapid progress but not towards general superintelligence](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/i-expect-rapid-progress-but-not-towards)

AI researcher Nathan Lambert shares his perspective on why he remains skeptical of near-term AGI predictions despite expecting continued rapid progress in AI capabilities. Lambert expresses surprise at how many top industry researchers believe AI will surpass human performance in their specific domains within years, suggesting a disconnect between expert predictions and his own assessment of current technological trajectories. This perspective matters because it represents a more measured view of AI progress timelines from someone closely observing industry developments, potentially informing more realistic planning for AI safety and governance measures.

## Policy & Governance

### [Lancet ranks nuclear war and AI among 17 major threats](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/oct/11/nuclear-war-and-malicious-use-of-ai-threaten-end-of-humanity-by-2100-says-lancet-assessment)

The prestigious medical journal The Lancet published a comprehensive threat assessment ranking nuclear war and malicious AI use among 17 major risks that could threaten human health and survival through 2100. A 35-member commission classified both nuclear conflict and harmful AI applications as potential extinction-level threats, though they deemed both scenarios unlikely, while also projecting significant health impacts from climate change, reduced international aid, and pollution. This assessment is significant because it represents mainstream medical and scientific recognition of AI as an existential risk category alongside established threats like nuclear weapons, potentially influencing public health policy and international risk mitigation efforts.
