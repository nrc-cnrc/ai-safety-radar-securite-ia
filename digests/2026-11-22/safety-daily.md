# Daily Digest (2026-11-22)

## Top 3 Developments

**International Negotiations on Autonomous Weapons ("Slaughterbots") Break Down** - The [Future of Life Institute](https://futureoflife.org/statement/negotiations-on-slaughterbots-conclude-with-frustration/) reports that formal multilateral talks on lethal autonomous weapons systems have concluded in frustration, with FLI signaling it will pursue the issue through alternative channels. This matters because it is a concrete setback for binding global governance of military AI, likely pushing the field toward a patchwork of coalition-based rules rather than a unified treaty.

**Study Finds Multimodal Image-Editing Systems Are "Vision-Blind" to Unsafe Inputs** - [Text-Aware, Vision-Blind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26569v1) introduces EditSafetyBench (5,940 image-instruction pairs) and shows that instruction-guided image-editing systems rely almost entirely on text-based safety filtering, failing to jointly assess risk when the uploaded image itself is unsafe. This matters because it identifies a structural safety gap as generative systems move from captioning toward richer editing pipelines with larger attack surfaces.

**Paper Argues Legal Infrastructure Is Lagging Behind Agent Communication Protocols** - [Law at the Speed of AI (AULA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25985v1) argues that technical agent-authentication protocols like MCP and Agent2Agent are maturing faster than the legal frameworks needed to define agents' scope of authority, proposing an "Autonomy Under Law Architecture" to close the gap. This matters because runtime technical controls alone cannot establish legal accountability for autonomous agents acting across organizational boundaries.
