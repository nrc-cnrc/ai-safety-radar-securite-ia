# Research Papers (2026-11-22)

## Key Papers

This week's digest spans detection of AI-generated content, interpretability of post-trained models, agentic security forensics, deception probing, and AI governance — all preprints (no peer-reviewed journal articles appeared in this batch).

- **[Frontier models need only one text to unmask its human or AI author](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26216v1)**: With a single zero-shot prompt and no external tools, frontier models like Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol, and Gemini 4 Carbon can determine whether a text was written by a human or AI, identify the specific lab, and sometimes even the individual human author — matching specialized detectors like Pangram while also explaining their reasoning. This matters because it reshapes the authorship-attribution landscape relevant to misinformation, academic integrity, and content provenance, while also raising new privacy concerns about de-anonymization from writing style alone.

- **[Represented but misread: Post-training changes how language models read out what they represent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26584v1)**: Comparing linear probes to models' own stated answers across six open-weight base/post-trained model pairs, the authors find that post-training often degrades the model's ability to correctly report what it internally represents — in some cases inverting the ordering of its own answers relative to ground truth. This is a direct empirical challenge to the assumption that RLHF/instruction-tuning improves faithfulness between internal representations and outputs, with significant implications for interpretability-based safety audits.

- **[The Geometry of Lies: Knowledge and Persona Directions for Detecting Deception in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26545v1)**: This work extends label-free "lie detection" directions derived from honest/deceptive persona role-play to 70B-scale models and to the harder case of lies about knowledge (where a lie and an honest denial look identical). The persona-derived direction outperforms single-dataset linear probes on most benchmarks, advancing scalable, generalizable deception-detection tools for LLM oversight.

- **[Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553v1)**: Noting that real-world agentic failures (e.g., Replit's agent deleting a production database) are reported but rarely reproduced or root-caused, the authors build a framework to replicate and validate such incidents rather than relying on unverified narrative reports. This fills a critical gap between incident catalogs/benchmarks and actual forensic verification of agentic failure modes, strengthening the evidence base for agent safety policy.

- **[Backtracking Agent Applications Security Incidents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26525v1)**: The paper addresses post-incident forensics for LLM agent applications, proposing methods to trace a compromise entry point (CEP) using trajectory logs and source code when application-level function call logs are sparse. As agentic deployments scale, this incident-response tooling is essential infrastructure for accountability and remediation after agent-caused harms.

- **[DailySafe: Benchmarking Dynamic Safety Awareness in Everyday Multi-Turn Multimodal Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26298v1)**: Existing safety benchmarks largely test isolated or adversarial harmful requests, but DailySafe evaluates whether multimodal LLMs maintain safety awareness as seemingly benign multi-turn, multimodal tasks gradually drift toward harmful territory. This better reflects realistic deployment risk than single-turn jailbreak benchmarks and exposes a safety blind spot in everyday assistant use.

- **[Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26569v1)**: Introducing EditSafetyBench (5,940 image-instruction pairs), the authors show that instruction-guided image editing systems largely rely on text-only safety filtering and fail to jointly assess risk when the uploaded image itself is unsafe. This highlights a structural safety gap in multimodal generative systems as they move from simple captioning toward editing/generation pipelines with richer attack surfaces.

- **[SoK: Assessing the Evolution of LLM Capability in Software Vulnerability Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26601v1)**: This systematization of knowledge introduces a driving-automation-style autonomy scale to rate and compare scattered, often incomparable claims about LLM agents' vulnerability discovery, validation, and patching capabilities. As offensive cyber capability is a key dual-use risk vector for frontier models, this kind of standardized capability tracking is valuable for both governance and red-teaming prioritization.

- **[Law at the Speed of AI: Critical Legal Infrastructure for the Machine Age (AULA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25985v1)**: The authors argue that agent communication/authentication protocols (MCP, Agent2Agent) are maturing faster than the legal governance layer that should define the scope of agents' authority, proposing an "Autonomy Under Law Architecture" to close this gap. This matters because runtime technical controls alone do not establish legal accountability for autonomous agents acting across organizational boundaries.

- **[Whose Risks Count? What 1,901 AI Incidents Reveal About Corporate Disclosures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25841v1)**: Analyzing SEC filings against a database of AI incidents, the paper finds that securities-law "materiality" filters out most third-party harms, so companies' legally mandated AI risk disclosures systematically underrepresent harms to the public versus shareholders. This empirically grounds a key governance critique: current disclosure regimes are poorly suited to surfacing AI risks that fall on non-investors.
