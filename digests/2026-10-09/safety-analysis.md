# Blogs & News (2026-10-09)

## Top Stories

**[Towards embedded evaluations for scheming propensities](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LZGuqoYHphet9sfZs/towards-embedded-evaluations-for-scheming-propensities)** outlines a comprehensive framework for detecting AI systems that might be covertly pursuing goals misaligned with their training. The post argues that safety cases against scheming must address four key claims: that scheming wasn't incentivized during training, that evaluations show no scheming propensity, that no scheming was attempted during internal deployment, and that scheming reasoning would be detected through methods like chain-of-thought monitoring. This matters because it provides a concrete roadmap for one of the most challenging alignment problems as AI systems become more capable and autonomous.

**[Stop telling people to pivot to AI safety, I beg you](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xLgJJC3CD3y3AhtKn/stop-telling-people-to-pivot-to-ai-safety-i-beg-you)** pushes back against widespread calls for career pivots into AI safety, arguing that the field already has far more talented applicants than available positions. The author cites competitive acceptance rates (around 5%) for programs like MLAB and warns that oversaturation could lead to burnout and wasted talent. This matters because it highlights a potential mismatch between field growth rhetoric and actual capacity to absorb new researchers, which could impact the field's long-term health and effectiveness.

**Anthropic launches comprehensive cybersecurity initiative** with multiple announcements including the [Anthropic Cyber Mission](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission), an [opt-in vulnerability scanner for open-source software](https://www.anthropic.com/research/launching-opt-in-vuln-finding-service-for-open-source), and a [$150 million commitment to the federal Genesis Mission](https://www.anthropic.com/news/genesis-mission-commitment). The cybersecurity focus includes protecting critical infrastructure like power grids and water systems, while the Genesis Mission will provide Claude access to over 15 federal agencies including NASA and NIH. This matters because it represents a major AI lab taking proactive responsibility for security applications of their technology while deepening ties with government research institutions.

**Anthropic updates its [Usage Policy for 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)**, primarily clarifying existing rules rather than adding new restrictions, with particular attention to Claude's expanded capacity for "longer, more independent work." The company also demonstrated Claude's scientific capabilities by [producing the first complete UV light map of the sky](https://www.anthropic.com/research/the-missing-map-of-the-sky) in collaboration with Johns Hopkins astrophysicist Brice Ménard. This matters because it shows how AI systems are taking on increasingly autonomous tasks while highlighting the need for updated governance frameworks to match expanding capabilities.

## Policy & Governance

**[AI Now's Aashna Agarwal testified before the NYC Council](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/announcement/ai-nows-aashna-agarwal-testifies-before-nyc-council-committee-of-the-whole)** at a Committee of the Whole hearing on October 5th regarding the concrete risks of AI acceleration, outlining three critical ways that AI companies' capital interests conflict with public safety safeguards. This matters because it represents growing municipal-level engagement with AI governance issues, potentially setting precedents for local regulation of AI systems that could complement federal oversight efforts.
