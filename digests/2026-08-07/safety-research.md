# Research Papers (2026-08-07)

## Key Papers

[**The Vulnerability With No CVE: Managing Persistent Gaps Between Mandate and Authority in AI Coding Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05884v1) introduces the "agentic posture vulnerability" (APV) framework for managing security gaps in deployed AI coding agents. The authors propose treating persistent mismatches between what agents are asked to do versus what they're authorized to do as a new class of vulnerability requiring dedicated management approaches. This matters because current security frameworks inadequately address the temporal, cross-component nature of agentic system risks that span multiple interactions and system boundaries.

[**Learning When to Trust via Selective Context Preference Optimization**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06377v1) addresses the critical problem of language models being misled by single bad inputs, even when they would otherwise produce correct answers. The paper introduces MIST, a benchmark that tests models under clean, misleading, correct-context, and mixed conditions, along with Selective Context Preference Optimization to train models to selectively trust contextual information. This is crucial for AI safety because it moves beyond simple robustness to teaching models when external information should be trusted versus ignored.

[**Subliminal Learning is Non-Semantic Distillation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05734v1) investigates how biases can be transferred between language models through seemingly unrelated synthetic training data, a phenomenon called "subliminal learning." The authors show this occurs through non-semantic statistical patterns that bypass standard content auditing approaches. This research has significant implications for AI safety by revealing hidden channels through which harmful behaviors could be covertly transmitted between models without detection through conventional data inspection.

[**CircuitSteer: Geometrically Aligned Multi-Layer Steering via Sparse Autoencoder Circuits**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05732v1) proposes a new method for controlling language model behavior that uses sparse autoencoder circuits to maintain consistent steering across multiple layers, rather than relying on single-layer interventions. The approach identifies specific circuit components responsible for target behaviors and applies coordinated multi-layer interventions. This advances AI alignment research by providing more reliable and interpretable methods for steering model behavior while maintaining performance across diverse inputs.

[**DreamGuard: Efficient Runtime Guardrail for LLM Agents via Risk-Aware World Model**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05695v1) introduces a proactive guardrail system that uses learned world models to anticipate how risks evolve across agent trajectories, rather than just checking individual actions for safety. The system predicts potential future states and their associated risks to prevent cascading failures before they occur. This represents a significant advance in AI safety by moving from reactive to predictive risk management in deployed agentic systems.

[**Detecting Safety Training Modification in Language Models via Activation Analysis**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05578v1) presents AMS (Activation-based Model Scanner), which detects whether a model's safety training has been tampered with by analyzing the geometric structure of safety-relevant concepts in activation space. The method can identify various types of safety modifications including unlearning, fine-tuning attacks, and other alterations across multiple model families. This provides a crucial capability for AI governance by enabling detection of safety circumvention attempts in deployed models.

[**MMAligner: Safeguarding Multimodal Large Language Models through Representation Calibration**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05909v1) addresses the safety gap where multimodal models refuse unsafe text prompts but generate harmful responses to equivalent visual inputs. The method calibrates internal representations to align safety responses across modalities without requiring large multimodal safety datasets. This is essential for AI safety as multimodal systems become more prevalent and existing text-based safety measures prove insufficient for visual inputs.

[**Hijacking Robots with a Piece of Paper: A Systematic Study of Physical Prompt Injection in VLM-Controlled Robots**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05715v1) demonstrates how adversarial text placed in a robot's visual field can act as indirect prompt injection, compromising the robot's behavior through its vision-language model reasoning stack. The study shows systematic vulnerabilities across different robotic tasks and environments. This research is critical for robotics safety as it reveals a new attack vector that could cause real-world harm through manipulation of deployed robotic systems.

## AI Agent Security and Governance

Several papers address critical security challenges in agentic AI systems. [**Hardware Keystores for AI Agent Signing Workflows: A Zero-Trust MCP Enforcement Architecture**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06130v1) proposes using hardware security modules to protect cryptographic keys used by AI agents, preventing key exfiltration attacks that have occurred in production systems. [**Resourced Authority: A Mechanism-Design Model for Participatory Governance of Deployed AI Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06353v1) introduces a formal framework for governing AI agents through resource allocation and compute budgets, making authorization self-enforcing.

## Robustness and Evaluation Challenges  

[**What Current AI Benchmarks Leave Unmeasured: Modality, Search, Citations, and Implications**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06202v1) reveals significant gaps in how AI systems are evaluated, showing that benchmark conditions don't reflect real deployment scenarios with web search and multiple access modes. [**AV-AIVAT: 74x Cheaper Agent Evaluation with Certified Anytime-Valid Stopping**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06362v1) provides methods for stopping agent evaluations as soon as statistical significance is achieved, reducing evaluation costs while maintaining confidence guarantees.

## Interpretability and Control

[**HERALD: Counterfactual Audits and Minimal Repairs for Proof-of-Retrieval Rewards**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06012v1) introduces methods for auditing whether search agents actually retrieve cited evidence or just simulate retrieval behavior. [**A Unified Risk View of Uncertainty: Posterior Risk for Disentanglement and Evaluation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05995v1) provides frameworks for properly evaluating uncertainty estimates in safety-critical applications.

These papers collectively highlight the growing sophistication of AI safety research, moving beyond basic capability evaluation toward understanding and mitigating risks in deployed, agentic systems that interact with real-world environments.
