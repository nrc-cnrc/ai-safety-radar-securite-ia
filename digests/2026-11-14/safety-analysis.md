# Blogs & News (2026-11-14)

## Top Stories

**[Building Strong Safety Evaluations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations)** — Anthropic published a detailed set of recommendations for designing, running, and reporting AI safety evaluations, drawn from its own internal experience building eval suites for frontier models. The piece covers common pitfalls (e.g., eval gaming, construct validity failures) and practical guidance for making evals more rigorous and reproducible. *Why it matters: As evaluations become the primary evidentiary basis for safety claims and regulatory compliance, methodological rigor in how they're built directly determines whether "passing an eval" actually means a model is safe.*

**Community reaction to METR's RSI/timelines findings** — Two LessWrong posts, [Psychologically Preparing for Short Timelines](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nKootiC6CuwuzLSou/psychologically-preparing-for-short-timelines) and [The Hackening: Justified Practical Advice Thread](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YA2r4rg6SpBtwRqtq/the-hackening-justified-practical-advice-and-open-questions), reflect a notable shift in community sentiment following recent METR research suggesting possible recursive self-improvement (RSI) signals and compressed 1-3 year AGI timelines. The posts function as crowdsourced "practical advice" threads (explicitly modeled on 2020 COVID-prep threads) covering both psychological coping and concrete preparedness questions. *Why it matters: Regardless of whether the underlying METR findings hold up, the fact that serious alignment researchers are treating short-timeline RSI scenarios as comparable in urgency to a pandemic onset is itself a signal worth tracking — it indicates eroding confidence in multi-year safety buffers.*

**[Open-source AI without Jensen](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-source-ai-without-jensen)** (Nathan Lambert) and **[AI #194: Spy Versus Guy](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-194-spy-versus-guy)** / **[Jensen Huang Resigns After Having Another Affair In Addition to the Chinese Spy](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/jensen-huang-resigns-after-having-having)** (Zvi Mowshowitz) — Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has resigned from both the CEO role and the board amid an espionage scandal and personal controversy. Lambert frames this as a "decapitation" event for the open-source AI ecosystem that has relied heavily on Nvidia's compute dominance and ecosystem leadership. *Why it matters: Nvidia's near-monopoly position makes it a single point of failure for compute governance, export-control enforcement, and open-model development; sudden leadership instability at this chokepoint has downstream implications for AI safety infrastructure, chip security, and the broader US-China compute competition.*

## Policy & Governance

**[AI Kill Switches: What Policymakers Should Know](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/ai-kill-switches-what-policymakers-should-know/)** (Georgetown CSET) — This explainer breaks down what an "AI kill switch" could mean in practice at each layer of the AI stack (hardware, model weights, deployment infrastructure, application layer), and offers policymakers a framework for evaluating proposals that mandate such mechanisms. *Why it matters: As legislators increasingly propose kill-switch requirements as a blunt safety instrument, this piece provides the technical grounding needed to avoid writing unenforceable or easily-circumvented mandates into law.*

**[AI Now Statement on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA)](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/announcement/ai-now-statement-on-the-bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act-baaja-to-us-congress)** (AI Now Institute) — AI Now issued a critical statement arguing that BAAJA functions as a "backdoor bailout" for the AI industry, noting that it comes despite recent voters electing representatives opposed to aggressive AI infrastructure buildout. *Why it matters: The statement highlights a widening gap between public sentiment on AI industrial policy and federal legislative action, a tension that will shape the feasibility of future AI safety and accountability regulation.*
