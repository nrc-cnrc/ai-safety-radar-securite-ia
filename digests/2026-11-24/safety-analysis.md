# Blogs & News (2026-11-24)

## Top Stories

- **[METR's new time horizon results, explained](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nCjAug5FgsCevAEGg/the-new-metr-time-horizon-results-basic-explainer)**: METR released updated "time horizon" measurements tracking how long a task AI systems can autonomously complete — a core proxy for measuring progress toward dangerous autonomous capabilities. This LessWrong explainer breaks down what the new plots show after the author found the raw release hard to parse despite working in the field. This matters because time-horizon trends are one of the few quantitative anchors the safety community has for forecasting when models cross capability thresholds relevant to risk assessments and deployment decisions.

- **[Anthropic: "Working with AI"](https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai)**: Anthropic interviewed people at 58 companies using Claude about how AI is reshaping work, finding few outright layoffs but significantly reduced hiring, with the most value coming from companies that reorganize roles and workflows around AI rather than simply bolting it onto existing jobs. This matters because it offers early empirical grounding for the "AI and labor markets" debate that usually relies on speculation, directly informing policy discussions about transition support and economic disruption.

- **[Comparing AI R&D compute caps and deployment lags](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/e758PJCpwAwyodvEj/comparing-ai-r-and-d-compute-caps-and-deployment-lags)**: This technical Alignment Forum post compares compute-cap policies against "blocking internal deployment" as pacing mechanisms for slowing dangerous capability gains, responding directly to a proposal from Anthropic's Evan Hubinger. This matters because it's a concrete attempt to formalize and stress-test governance mechanisms (compute reallocation limits vs. deployment gating) that labs may actually adopt in risk-assessment frameworks.

- **[AISN #86: Anthropic Accused of Recklessness as it Goes Public](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-86-anthropic-accused-of-recklessness)**: CAIS's newsletter covers criticism of Anthropic's move toward going public, alongside new loss-of-control lawsuits being filed against AI companies and Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO. This matters because it signals growing legal and reputational pressure on frontier labs, with "recklessness" framings and loss-of-control litigation potentially becoming a major governance lever independent of formal regulation.

## Policy & Governance

- **[Judge the Cyber Apocalypse by the Data, Not the Headlines](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/judge-the-cyber-apocalypse-by-the-data-not-the-headlines/)** (Georgetown CSET): CSET argues that while technical metrics show AI increasingly favoring attackers over defenders in cybersecurity, real-world impact data tells a less alarming story than headline narratives about a "vulnpocalypse" suggest — implying attribution and enforcement may matter as much as raw attacker capability. This matters because it pushes policymakers to ground cyber-risk regulation in outcome data rather than capability-based fear, which could shape how AI cyber risk is prioritized relative to other threat models.

- **[AISN #86: Anthropic Accused of Recklessness as it Goes Public](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-86-anthropic-accused-of-recklessness)** (CAIS): Beyond the Anthropic IPO controversy, this issue flags new loss-of-control lawsuits against AI companies — an emerging accountability mechanism distinct from regulatory action. This matters because litigation could establish legal precedent on AI harm liability well before comprehensive AI legislation exists in most jurisdictions.
