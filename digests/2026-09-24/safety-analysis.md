# Blogs & News (2026-09-24)

## Top Stories

**[Claude discovers a novel enzyme system](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system)** - Anthropic announced the launch of a new life sciences research group that uses Claude to analyze DNA datasets, identify uncharacterized protein families, and generate testable hypotheses. In their first major result, Claude discovered a novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats through computational analysis that was then validated through laboratory experiments. This represents a significant milestone in AI-assisted scientific discovery and demonstrates how advanced AI systems can contribute to fundamental biological research.

**[MIRI's Position on the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act of 2026](https://intelligence.org/2026/09/23/miris-position-on-the-ban-artificial-superintelligence-act-of-2026/)** - MIRI released their official position on proposed federal legislation to ban artificial superintelligence development, marking a significant policy intervention from the AI safety research institute that has been warning about superintelligence risks for over two decades. The statement, endorsed by Eliezer Yudkowsky and other MIRI leadership, represents the organization's most direct engagement with specific legislative proposals. This signals growing alignment between technical AI safety researchers and policy approaches that treat superintelligence development as an existential risk requiring regulatory intervention.

**[Why I'm scared of RL](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/LcQ9x72eNji2gpS9b/why-i-m-scared-of-rl)** - A comprehensive analysis on the Alignment Forum argues that reinforcement learning represents a "black-box source of agency" that poses fundamental misalignment risks, especially compared to scaffolding-based approaches to AI capability. The author expresses concern about recent incidents and the trajectory of RL development, particularly worried about environments that incorporate multiple agents leading to emergent behaviors. This reflects growing technical concerns within the alignment community about current approaches to training advanced AI systems.

**[WorkspaceBench: Evaluating Interpretability Methods for the Global Workspace](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/Zeg2JztbdhguL48uH/workspacebench-evaluating-interpretability-methods-for-the)** - Researchers introduced a new benchmark with 3,356 questions across 27 evaluation families to assess how well interpretability tools can decode the "global workspace" of AI models during inference. The benchmark specifically focuses on minimizing hallucinations in interpretability techniques and spans safety-relevant tasks, logical reasoning, and multihop computation. This provides a much-needed standardized evaluation framework for mechanistic interpretability research, which is crucial for developing reliable methods to understand advanced AI systems.

**[Claude Opus 5.5: The System Card](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-opus-55-the-system-card)** - Zvi Mowshowitz analyzes the release of Claude Opus 5.5, which is being positioned as the world's most powerful AI model according to benchmarks from Artificial Analysis and other standard evaluations. The analysis focuses on Anthropic's system card and safety evaluations for this new model release. This represents another significant capability jump in frontier AI systems and raises questions about the pace of development relative to safety measures.

## Policy & Governance

**US and China urged to agree safety tests** - Multiple stakeholders including 22 governments, the UN, and OpenAI are pushing for international AI safety standards ahead of a planned US-China summit, according to [reporting](https://www.heise.de/news/Vor-US-chinesischem-KI-Gipfel-Wer-alles-internationale-Regulierungen-fordert-11461405.html?wt_mc=rss.red.ho.ho.atom.beitrag.beitrag) from German tech publication Heise. The coordinated push includes calls for independent testing, shared technical standards, and common safety protocols for powerful AI systems, though major powers have not yet made firm commitments. This coordination effort represents the most significant international diplomatic push for AI safety standards to date and could establish precedents for global AI governance frameworks.
