# Blogs & News (2026-10-04)

## Top Stories

**[What I learnt co-leading an AI Safety bootcamp for legal and governance practitioners](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KtAug62dYRgAS8sqJ/what-i-learnt-co-leading-an-ai-safety-bootcamp-for-legal-and)** - A practitioner reflection on training legal and governance professionals in AI safety, noting that while AI safety has gained mainstream attention and policy wins, political will and market incentives remain key bottlenecks rather than technical knowledge gaps. This highlights the growing recognition that AI safety implementation requires interdisciplinary expertise beyond just technical research.

**[You can't use it without becoming like me](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WyeYJ7cmeiSrXeCnM/you-can-t-use-it-without-becoming-like-me)** - An analysis of "fast following loops" in warfare where adversaries rapidly copy and adapt each other's innovations, drawing parallels to how AI capabilities and safety measures might spread between competing nations and organizations. This underscores concerns about how competitive dynamics could accelerate AI development timelines and complicate coordination on safety measures.

**[The AI Preference Cascade Reaches Farther](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-ai-preference-cascade-reaches)** - Commentary on the expanding recognition of AI risks among experts and how this awareness is cascading through broader communities. This suggests growing consensus among informed observers about AI safety concerns, potentially creating momentum for stronger safety measures.

## Policy & Governance

**[China has cracked down on AI relationships. Is it ahead of the game?](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/china-has-cracked-down-on-ai-relationships-is-it-ahead-of-the-game/)** - Georgetown CSET analysis of China's new regulations on AI companions, examining the country's approach to addressing emotional dependency, child safety, and broader social effects of human-like AI systems. This represents an early example of how nations might regulate AI systems that create deep emotional or psychological relationships with users.

**[What America and China Fear Most About AI](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/what-america-and-china-fear-most-about-ai/)** - CSET discussion featuring Helen Toner on U.S.-China competition and cooperation in artificial intelligence, exploring mutual concerns and potential areas for collaboration. This analysis is crucial for understanding how great power competition might shape global AI governance and safety coordination efforts.
