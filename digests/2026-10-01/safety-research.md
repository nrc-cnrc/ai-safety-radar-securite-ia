# Research Papers (2026-10-01)

## Key Papers

Several critical developments emerged this week in AI safety and alignment research, with peer-reviewed work taking precedence over preprints on similar topics.

**[Pastoral AI ethics for chatbots in religious education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10676-026-09924-y)** introduces a framework integrating AI ethics with pastoral values for educational AI deployment. The work addresses privacy, transparency, fairness, and accountability alongside doctrinal fidelity and human dignity. This matters as it demonstrates domain-specific ethical frameworks needed for responsible AI deployment in sensitive contexts.

**[Preemptive LLM Unlearning against Forbidden Capability Acquisition via Gradient Sealing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39866v1)** presents a proactive approach to prevent LLMs from acquiring dangerous capabilities during fine-tuning, using gradient manipulation to "seal" forbidden knowledge acquisition paths. Unlike retrospective unlearning that removes existing capabilities, this preemptive method blocks harmful capability development. This is crucial for maintaining safety guardrails as open-weight models become more widespread and susceptible to adversarial fine-tuning.

**[CodeMimicry: Exploiting Safety Generalization Lag in Large Language Models via Structured Code Completion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39902v1)** identifies a critical vulnerability where safety alignment trained on natural language fails to transfer effectively to code domains. The authors demonstrate how malicious intent embedded in syntactically valid code can evade safety mechanisms through this "safety generalization lag." This reveals a fundamental gap in current alignment approaches that could enable sophisticated jailbreaking attacks.

**[Learning Steganography Is Easy, Learning Steganographic Reasoning Is Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39838v1)** distinguishes between simple steganographic messaging and more complex steganographic reasoning in LLMs, showing that while models can easily hide messages, developing coherent hidden reasoning processes remains challenging. This finding is important for understanding the limits of deceptive capabilities in current models and informing oversight strategies.

**[FIGS: Evaluating Multi-Turn Sycophancy Without Penalizing Empathy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39863v1)** introduces a nuanced evaluation framework that separates harmful sycophantic behavior from legitimate empathetic responses across conversational turns. Current evaluations fail to distinguish between appropriate supportiveness and problematic agreement with false claims. This work provides crucial tools for measuring alignment quality without discouraging beneficial empathetic capabilities.

**[Unlearning Deceptive Behaviors in LLMs with Contrastive Forget Sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38909v1)** proposes targeting deceptive behaviors rather than factual knowledge during machine unlearning, using contrastive examples to preserve truthful capabilities while removing context-dependent deception. This approach addresses a key limitation of existing unlearning methods that can impair legitimate model functionality. The work is essential for developing more precise safety interventions.

**[Trust Is Not a Score: Runtime Assurance Contracts for High-Risk AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39717v1)** introduces Runtime Assurance Contracts (RACs) that formally bind autonomy boundaries to observed evidence during consequential tasks, moving beyond simple confidence scores to structured policy-level controls. The framework addresses the critical gap between pre-deployment evaluation and runtime safety assurance. This matters because it provides a rigorous foundation for deploying AI agents in high-stakes scenarios while maintaining human oversight and control.

**[How Much Is an AI Token Worth? Scaling Laws for Wild AI-Generated Web Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40295v1)** analyzes the impact of naturally occurring AI-generated content in web training data, finding 27.5-31.1% of recent web tokens are AI-generated and establishing scaling laws for mixed human-AI training corpora. This research addresses a critical emerging challenge as AI-generated content increasingly dominates training datasets, with implications for model collapse and training data quality.
