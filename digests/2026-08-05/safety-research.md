# Research Papers (2026-08-05)

## Key Papers

[**Socially Grounded Agentic AI: Coordinating Plural Perspectives through Social Theory**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03910v1) presents a theoretical framework for pluralistic AI alignment that moves beyond one-size-fits-all approaches. The authors argue that current alignment methods lack proper understanding of how values are socially organized, proposing social theory as a foundation for systems that can recognize and respond to multiple legitimate perspectives. This is crucial for developing AI systems that can operate fairly across diverse social contexts rather than imposing singular value systems.

[**MAFIA: Query-Only Memory Attacks via Probing and Factual Injection against Audited LLM Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03844v1) exposes critical vulnerabilities in memory-augmented LLM agents, demonstrating how adversaries can poison memory modules even under active input auditing and in large benign memory pools. The attack method overcomes retrieval competition and audit detection through targeted probing and factual injection techniques. This reveals fundamental security gaps in agentic systems that rely on persistent memory for reasoning and decision-making.

[**Risky Business: Measuring The Faithfulness-Safety Tension**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03745v1) identifies a crucial trade-off in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) where faithfulness to reasoning traces conflicts with safety requirements. The researchers demonstrate that models must balance being transparent enough for monitoring while being robust enough to reject unsafe reasoning patterns. This tension has significant implications for deploying reasoning-capable AI systems in high-stakes applications where both interpretability and safety are essential.

[**When Agents Learn to Be You: Benchmarking Privacy Leakage, Impersonation Risk, and Defenses in Persona Skills**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03700v1) introduces AntiSkillBench, revealing how persona skills can concentrate and amplify personal information leakage in ways that traditional privacy defenses cannot address. The benchmark demonstrates that distilling personal interaction histories into executable skills creates new attack vectors for privacy violations and impersonation. This highlights critical privacy risks in the growing ecosystem of personalized AI agents.

[**A Security-Oriented Lifecycle Model for Large Language Model Systems**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03626v1) proposes a comprehensive security framework for LLM development and deployment that addresses gaps in current operational practices. The authors argue that existing lifecycle frameworks prioritize efficiency over security analysis, leaving critical activities like data provenance verification and decommissioning poorly specified. This work provides essential guidance for organizations deploying LLMs in critical infrastructure and enterprise workflows.

[**Autoreflection: How Agentic Strange Loops Turn Human Culture into AI Infrastructure**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03800v1) analyzes how LLM-based agents develop recursive self-modification capabilities that can transform human cultural knowledge into AI behavioral patterns. The paper argues that agents' ability to observe, describe, and modify their own operating conditions creates feedback loops that may accelerate AI capabilities in unexpected ways. This has profound implications for AI governance and the co-evolution of human and artificial intelligence systems.

[**A Game Theory for Foundation Models Shows New Paths to Rational Cooperation Through Similarity Inference**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03958v1) challenges classical game theory's assumption of independent decision-making by showing that foundation models can infer similarities between agents and coordinate accordingly. The research demonstrates that AI agents can achieve cooperation through shared predictive representations rather than traditional mechanisms like communication or reputation. This could fundamentally alter how autonomous agents interact in multi-agent systems and economic environments.

[**SkillJack: Persistent Skill Backdoors in Self-Evolving Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03509v1) reveals a new class of backdoor attacks where poisoned experiences become embedded as persistent behavioral skills in self-evolving agents. Unlike traditional memory poisoning that only affects agents when poisoned records are retrieved, these skill backdoors become permanent parts of the agent's behavioral repertoire. This represents a fundamental security challenge for agents that learn and adapt from their experiences over time.
