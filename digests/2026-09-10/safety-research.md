# Research Papers (2026-09-10)

## Key Papers

**[Active Adaptation, Not Static Defense: Temporal Dynamics of Preventative Steering in Adversarial Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10142v1)** analyzes how Preventative Steering protects language models from harmful persona drift during malicious fine-tuning by injecting undesirable-trait persona vectors during training and removing them at evaluation time. The research reveals that defense emerges through an early compensatory adaptation phase followed by a steady-state phase where corrective signals persist even after vector removal. This work provides crucial insights into training-time defenses against adversarial fine-tuning attacks.

**[Forgetting Only What Matters: Layer-Selective Unlearning toward Robust LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10439v1)** proposes a targeted approach to machine unlearning that selectively updates only the model layers most critical for encoding unwanted knowledge, rather than applying broad parameter updates. The method shows improved robustness under deployment changes like post-training quantization, where forgotten knowledge might otherwise re-emerge. This addresses a key challenge in making unlearning techniques practical for real-world LLM deployments.

**[How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793v1)** demonstrates that directional ablation attacks—which remove refusal capabilities by projecting out a single "refusal direction"—remain effective even on frontier-scale mixture-of-experts models with 320B parameters. This white-box attack requires no gradient optimization and only hundreds of contrastive prompts, yet successfully breaks safety alignment on very large models. The finding suggests that alignment robustness may not automatically improve with scale.

**[DiSCo: A Distribution-First Steering and Cultural Prior Evaluation Framework for Measuring Cultural Preference Bias in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10253v1)** introduces a framework for evaluating cultural bias in LLMs when multiple culturally grounded responses are valid, moving beyond single "correct" answer evaluation. The approach separates knowledge gaps from cultural preference biases and provides methods for steering models toward more culturally balanced responses. This work addresses critical fairness concerns as LLMs are deployed globally across diverse cultural contexts.

**[Through the Looking Glass: Directly Reading and Writing Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10210v1)** analyzes transformer decision-making by examining which components contribute to token predictions, finding that while thousands of components influence predictions, only dozens are truly critical—53 components carry 90% of a prediction's weight, with just 13 being survival-critical. The research reveals that the mass pushing away from predicted tokens is typically seven times larger than the mass supporting them, providing new insights into transformer interpretability.

**[A Dominant Diffuse Phase in the Sparse Autoencoder Phase Diagram](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10299v1)** investigates when sparse autoencoders (SAEs) successfully recover true features versus when distinct features become merged, implementing a controlled experiment varying nesting fraction, sparsity penalty, and dictionary size. The study maps out phase transitions in SAE behavior and identifies a dominant "diffuse phase" where feature recovery fails, providing important guidance for mechanistic interpretability research using SAEs.

**[Subgroup Membership Inference Audits of Differentially Private Synthetic Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09848v1)** extends membership inference attack audits to focus on subgroup-specific privacy risks in differentially private text synthesis, going beyond average-case analysis to examine whether certain demographic groups face disproportionate privacy exposure. This work reveals that aggregate privacy metrics can mask significant subgroup vulnerabilities, highlighting the need for more nuanced privacy evaluation frameworks.

**[Beyond Training: A Feasibility Taxonomy for Inference-Time AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10105v1)** analyzes governance mechanisms that apply during model deployment rather than training, as AI capabilities increasingly emerge through inference-time scaling and agentic scaffolding. The paper provides a systematic taxonomy of inference-time governance approaches, addressing a critical gap as regulatory focus has primarily targeted training compute thresholds rather than deployment-stage risks.
