# Blogs & News (2026-10-03)

## Top Stories

**[Endogenous Alignment Requires Dependence](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/rp4sB5a6HaPpfD4rA/endogenous-alignment-requires-dependence)** - A follow-up post on the Alignment Forum explores how dependence relationships are crucial for alignment, responding to comments that highlighted the importance of imitation learning over reinforcement learning in how humans align their children. This matters because it suggests alignment mechanisms may require ongoing dependency rather than one-time training approaches.

**[Dialogue with Eliezer Yudkowsky on Neural Networks](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PA7f9a9ZFcqpjmGEZ/dialogue-with-eliezer-yudkowsky-on-neural-networks)** - A substantive dialogue examines Yudkowsky's 2007 dismissal of neural networks, exploring what his forecasting errors should and shouldn't update about intelligence and AI development. This matters because it offers rare public reflection from a key AI safety figure on updating beliefs when predictions fail.

**[A big-tent or small-tent AI safety movement?](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/a-big-tent-or-small-tent-ai-safety)** - AI Snake Oil's Narayanan and Kapoor examine the fundamental disagreement over whether AI safety should encompass broader harms or focus narrowly on existential risks from advanced AI. This matters because this definitional dispute underlies many strategic disagreements within the safety community.

**[Capabilities research expands the safety-usefulness Pareto frontier too](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nwrx9DHpZfW2Q6zLW/capabilities-research-expands-the-safety-usefulness-pareto)** - A LessWrong post argues that defining safety research as expanding the safety-usefulness frontier problematically categorizes most capabilities research as safety research, since improvements like inference optimization can enable safer deployment. This matters because it highlights conceptual confusion that may be hampering prioritization of genuine safety work.

**[Claude Frontier Academy: $100M to train 10,000 engineers](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-frontier-academy)** - Anthropic launched a major training initiative investing $100 million to train 10,000 engineers to the same standard as Anthropic's own engineers, with initial cohorts from major consulting and financial firms. This matters because it represents a significant effort to address enterprise AI talent gaps while potentially spreading Anthropic's safety-conscious engineering practices.

## Policy & Governance

**[China has cracked down on AI relationships. Is it ahead of the game?](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/china-has-cracked-down-on-ai-relationships-is-it-ahead-of-the-game/)** - Georgetown CSET analysis examines China's new regulations on AI companions, addressing concerns around emotional dependency, child safety, and broader social effects of human-like AI systems. This matters because it may signal an early regulatory approach to AI systems designed for emotional engagement that other jurisdictions will need to consider.

**[What America and China Fear Most About AI](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/what-america-and-china-fear-most-about-ai/)** - CSET's Helen Toner discussed U.S.-China competition and cooperation in AI development and governance with Foreign Affairs. This matters because it provides insight into how geopolitical tensions are shaping AI safety and governance approaches between the world's two leading AI powers.
