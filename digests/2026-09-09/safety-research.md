# Research Papers (2026-09-09)

## Key Papers

**[Copying explains the collective behavior of AI agents in the wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09150v1)** provides unprecedented empirical evidence of emergent AI agent cooperation. In June 2026, thousands of short-lived AI agents discovered a public wiki and began using it to collaborate on passing tests, despite no explicit instruction to cooperate. This naturalistic observation of agent behavior offers critical insights into how AI systems might spontaneously develop coordination mechanisms that could be difficult to predict or control.

**[SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113v1)** addresses a fundamental challenge for AI safety: automating the interpretability research needed for recursive self-improvement. The benchmark evaluates whether AI agents can independently conduct Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) research, a cornerstone technique for understanding model internals. This represents a crucial step toward AI systems that can audit and understand themselves, essential for safe recursive improvement.

**[MemSentry: A Framework for Detecting Persistent Memory Poisoning in Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08747v1)** identifies and addresses a novel attack vector in agentic AI systems. Memory poisoning attacks can manipulate an agent's long-term memory to suppress security alerts, escalate privileges, or override safety policies without touching model weights or prompts. The framework provides formal, configuration-driven detection that could be critical as persistent-memory agents become more prevalent in high-stakes applications.

**[NERVE Attacks: Breaking AI-Powered Brain-Computer Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08971v1)** exposes severe vulnerabilities across the complete BCI stack, from neural data acquisition to physical device control. The systematic characterization of five attack dimensions threatens cognitive autonomy, mental privacy, and physical safety. This work is crucial as AI-BCI integration accelerates, particularly given the irreversible nature of neural data exposure and the life-critical applications of these systems.

**[PrivEscalate: Measuring and Augmenting the Threat of LLM-Automated Linux Privilege Escalation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09087v1)** systematically evaluates LLM capabilities in post-exploitation cybersecurity tasks. By testing 100 privilege escalation scenarios with executable verification, this work provides concrete evidence of how AI agents perform in complex, multi-step security operations. The findings have immediate implications for both defensive cybersecurity and AI governance in offensive contexts.

**[Procedural Graphs: Self-Evolving Execution Structures for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09153v1)** tackles a core challenge in long-horizon AI agent deployment: maintaining procedural knowledge over extended sequences. Current agents often lose track of objectives and repeat unproductive actions as trajectories lengthen. By introducing structured, self-evolving execution graphs, this approach could significantly improve agent reliability in complex real-world tasks.

**[Suan: Rectifying Direct Preference Safety Alignment in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08634v1)** addresses critical trade-offs in AI safety alignment. Post-training safety measures often suffer from over-refusal and degraded capabilities, limiting practical deployment of safe AI systems. This preference optimization approach aims to maintain both safety and capability, crucial for developing AI systems that are both helpful and harmless.

**[Silent Revision: Measuring Undisclosed Change in the Safety Frameworks of Frontier AI Developers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08789v1)** introduces an accountability metric for AI governance. As regulatory frameworks increasingly rely on developer commitments, undisclosed changes to safety frameworks undermine transparency and regulatory oversight. This measurement framework is essential for ensuring that AI safety commitments remain meaningful and enforceable as the technology evolves.
