# Daily Digest (2026-10-29)

## Top 3 Developments

**Mechanistic Studies Show Compression Can Silently Break Safety-Relevant Circuits** - Two companion papers, [Mechanistic Failure Modes of Weight Pruning in Transformer Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37126v1) and [What Survives Distillation? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157v1), use circuit-level analysis to show that pruning and distillation can preserve aggregate accuracy while substantially disrupting the underlying computational circuits models rely on. This matters because widely deployed "efficient" model variants may silently lose the mechanistic integrity that interpretability and alignment techniques assume, undermining the transferability of safety guarantees from base to compressed models.

**Concordat Proposes Enforceable State-Machine Protocols for Agent Actions** - [Concordat: Typed Sessions for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37461v1) enforces agent action protocols as finite-state machines rather than relying on prompt-only instructions, part of a broader trend toward structural, enforceable safeguards for consequential agent actions. This matters because prompt-based controls are brittle under adversarial or edge-case conditions, and typed, structurally enforced protocols offer a more robust foundation for increasingly autonomous tool-using agents.

**Anthropic Expands AI for Science Program to 20 Institutions** - [Anthropic announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ai-for-science-expands-foundations-research-institutions) an expansion of its AI for Science initiative to 20 scientific foundations, institutions, and research groups worldwide. This matters because it reflects labs' growing efforts to channel frontier capability toward verifiable, beneficial applications, offering a counter-narrative to catastrophic-risk framing just as Anthropic pursues a high-profile IPO.
