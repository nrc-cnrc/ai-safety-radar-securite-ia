# Research Papers (2026-10-29)

## Key Papers

This week's arXiv output included no peer-reviewed journal articles directly focused on AI safety or alignment; the lone journal paper identified ([Revealing nonlinear and interaction mechanisms of resource and environmental carrying capacity through explainable machine learning](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0360548), PLOS ONE) applies explainable ML to environmental policy rather than AI risk, so this digest focuses on the strongest preprint contributions across security, interpretability, alignment, and agent safety.

- **[Heads of Security: Learned Attention Gates Improve Prompt Injection Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37657v1)**: Researchers from a cross-industry team (including collaborators from Max Planck and ELLIS institutes) introduce HoS, a defense that learns per-attention-head gates to enforce instruction-data separation, allowing models to process untrusted content without executing embedded instructions. Evaluated on Llama-3.3-70B and GPT-OSS-20B against strong baselines (SecAlign++, ASIDE), it offers a lightweight, architecture-level alternative to finetuning-based defenses. This matters because prompt injection remains one of the most practically exploited vulnerabilities in deployed LLM agents, and head-level interventions could generalize better than prompt- or data-level fixes.

- **[Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481v1)**: This paper presents SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient-embedding method that identifies seemingly benign fine-tuning examples whose gradients nonetheless push models toward unsafe behavior, evading standard content filters. By restricting backpropagation to the final transformer layers, the method makes large-scale data attribution for safety degradation tractable. This is significant because "silent" safety erosion during instruction tuning is a known but poorly understood risk, and this offers a practical forensic tool for auditing fine-tuning datasets before deployment.

- **[Watermark Basins: Black-Box Detection of Distilled LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37266v1)**: A large industry team (including Meta-affiliated researchers) proposes an improved black-box method for detecting when a student model has been distilled from a watermarked proprietary teacher, moving beyond fragile token-level statistics to capture "radioactive" signals that persist through distillation. This addresses a growing governance concern: as API-based distillation of frontier models proliferates, model providers need robust forensic tools to enforce usage terms and attribute capability leakage.

- **[Uncovering Why LLMs Fail: From Internal Mechanisms to Adversarial Reasoning Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37369v1)**: The authors introduce CauTrace, a white-box diagnostic framework that traces how reasoning failures — both ordinary and adversarially induced — develop internally over a model's reasoning trajectory, rather than relying on endpoint correctness/safety labels. Unifying benign and adversarial failure analysis under common internal coordinates is valuable for building more rigorous interpretability-based safety evaluations that go beyond black-box behavioral testing.

- **[Mechanistic Failure Modes of Weight Pruning in Transformer Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37126v1)** and **[What Survives Distillation? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157v1)**: Both from the same authors, these companion studies use circuit-level analysis (the Indirect Object Identification circuit) to show that compression techniques — pruning and distillation — can preserve aggregate accuracy/perplexity while substantially disrupting the underlying computational circuits. This matters for safety because widely deployed "efficient" models may silently lose the mechanistic integrity that interpretability and alignment techniques assume, undermining transferability of safety guarantees from base to compressed models.

- **[Orthogonal and Parametrized Multi-Attribute Steering of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37517v1)** and **[CANS: Capability-Preserving Neuron Steering for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35530v1)**: These two papers advance activation steering — a lightweight, training-free alignment tool — by making steering strength more interpretable and controllable (via per-vector steerable intervals) and by explicitly balancing behavioral control against preservation of general capabilities. Together they push steering techniques toward becoming more reliable, calibrated levers for runtime behavior control, a key capability for scalable oversight and deployment-time safety interventions.

- **[CASEL: Consequence-Aware Selective Execution for Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36570v1)** and **[Concordat: Typed Sessions for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37461v1)**: CASEL proposes a runtime policy that allocates scrutiny (direct execution, verification, sandboxing, clarification) to agentic tool calls based on calibrated failure probability, consequence severity, and reversibility, while Concordat enforces agent action protocols as finite-state machines rather than relying on prompt-only instructions. Both represent a maturing trend toward structural, enforceable safeguards for increasingly autonomous, tool-using LLM agents that take consequential real-world actions (refunds, account changes, etc.).

- **[Aligning Large Language Models for Safety in African Languages: A Case Study of Hausa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37245v1)**: The authors build HausaSafeLM, one of the first safety preference datasets for Hausa (spoken by 200M+ people), derived from PKU-SafeRLHF via human correction and machine translation, and evaluate how existing alignment methods transfer to this low-resource, culturally distinct setting. This highlights a critical equity gap in AI safety research, which remains overwhelmingly concentrated on English and other high-resource languages, leaving large populations with weaker safety guarantees.

- **[Teach reality, not ethics: a drop-in continued-pretraining corpus installs coupled human-biosphere-AI welfare dispositions across model families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37484v1)**: This paper constructs a continued-pretraining corpus emphasizing documented human-biosphere-AI interdependence and irreversibility risk, aiming to instill dispositions against irreversible harm to living systems without explicit ethical instruction. It's a notable attempt to shape model values through factual/ecological framing rather than rules-based RLHF, relevant to debates about how deeply and durably value alignment can be embedded via pretraining data choices.

- **[Risk-Aware Output Control Should be the Focus of Machine Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35052v1)**: The authors argue that machine unlearning research has misaligned its core objective — targeting parameter-space indistinguishability from retrained models — when the practically relevant goal is controlling risky outputs (leakage, toxicity, bias) at inference time. This reframing matters for regulatory contexts (e.g., GDPR "right to be forgotten" compliance) where output-level guarantees, not internal weight similarity, are what actually protects users.
