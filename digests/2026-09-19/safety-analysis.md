# Blogs & News (2026-09-19)

## Top Stories

**[Anthropic Partners with Accenture on Embedded Evaluation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation)** - Anthropic announced a partnership with Accenture's specialist AI business Faculty to conduct independent evaluation of frontier AI systems, including model evaluation, red-teaming, alignment assessments, and safeguard testing. This represents a concrete implementation of the "embedded evaluators" commitment made in Anthropic CEO's recent essay "We Must Pace the Frontier." This matters because it demonstrates how major AI labs are beginning to implement third-party oversight mechanisms for frontier model development.

**[Stopgap Measures to Address Immediate AI Security Threats](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LqBAxFdyAiybnPL8e/stopgap-measures-to-address-immediate-ai-security-threats)** - A LessWrong post outlines urgent security measures needed as AI companies race toward superintelligent systems that could autonomously overpower national security forces, arguing that no entity currently knows how to control such systems once developed. The post emphasizes that current AI development is explicitly aimed at creating systems that can fully replace and outmatch humans in all domains, including hacking and military operations. This matters because it highlights the gap between AI capabilities development and security preparedness at a critical juncture.

**[The Preference Cascade Is Only Getting Started](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-preference-cascade-is-only-getting)** - Zvi Mowshowitz analyzes what he describes as an emerging preference cascade around AI existential risk, following up on his previous post about recent developments including Jacob Coxon's resignation that have "escalated quickly." The analysis suggests a shift in public and expert opinion about AI risks is accelerating. This matters because preference cascades can rapidly reshape policy landscapes and funding priorities in AI safety.

**[A Defense of Gradual Disempowerment](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/jXBmrEQj7zKGgiaYh/a-defense-of-gradual-disempowerment)** - An Alignment Forum post defends the "Gradual Disempowerment" theory from a 2025 paper, which proposes that existential risk from AI could arise through competitive displacement of humans across societal functions rather than through traditional misalignment scenarios. The post responds to critiques and argues this represents a distinct pathway to loss of human influence. This matters because it expands the taxonomy of AI existential risks beyond alignment failures to include economic and social displacement scenarios.

## Policy & Governance

**[AI Now Institute Commentary on Recent Security Incidents](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/press/hugging-face-hack-shows-humans-can-keep-ai-in-check)** - Following recent hacks at OpenAI and Hugging Face, AI Now's Heidy Khlaaf argued that "ordinary security engineering would have stopped this well short" of reaching sensitive data, while Sarah Myers West called for clarity in breaking down actual AI risks rather than responding to panic about capabilities. This matters because it represents a measured response to recent security incidents that could inform more targeted regulatory approaches.

**[AI Now Director Testifies on AI Monopolization](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/announcement/ai-nows-co-executive-director-amba-kak-testifies-before-monopoly-busters-caucus)** - AI Now Co-Executive Director Amba Kak testified before the Monopoly Busters Caucus at a shadow hearing on "AI Crossroads: Stopping Big Tech's Race to Monopolize AI through Surveillance," addressing concerns about market concentration amid increased focus on existential AI risks. This matters because it highlights the intersection of competition policy and AI safety governance as regulatory attention intensifies.

**[UN Secretary-General Calls for Binding AI Summit](https://news.un.org/feed/view/en/story/2026/09/1168353)** - UN Secretary-General Guterres called for binding international agreements on AI, announcing the creation of an Independent International Scientific Panel and Global Dialogue to coordinate policy on AI safety, human rights, and access. The initiative aims to establish shared AI principles and global standards. This matters because it represents a significant escalation in international coordination efforts on AI governance at a critical moment in AI development.

**[South Korea Announces National AI Rollout](https://asiatimes.com/2026/09/why-south-korea-is-giving-51-million-people-unlimited-ai)** - South Korea announced plans to provide all 51 million residents with free, unlimited access to AI chatbots while building domestic AI capabilities and implementing strict AI regulations, combining economic development goals with public service delivery and safety measures. This matters because it represents the first major national-scale AI deployment coupled with comprehensive regulatory frameworks, potentially serving as a model for other countries.
