# Blogs & News (2026-11-13)

## Top Stories

1. **[Orienting to 1 year AGI timelines](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/biFHYdJnMSCmW9Xou/orienting-to-1-year-agi-timelines)** — This LessWrong post argues that, absent deliberate slowdown, timelines to superintelligence have compressed sharply, with a median estimate around early 2028 and AI substantially automating AI research as early as 2027. The author contends that if leading labs don't solve alignment before an intelligence explosion, averting catastrophe may require an aggressive pacing agreement or outright pause within the next 6-12 months, and that interventions enabling such coordination are now the highest-leverage safety actions. This matters because it crystallizes a growing view within the alignment community that the window for voluntary, bottom-up safety research is closing faster than previously assumed, raising the stakes for governance and pause-type interventions.

2. **[Fable and Mythos 5.2: The System Card](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/fable-and-mythos-52-the-system-card)** — Zvi Mowshowitz's commentary digs into the system card accompanying a new frontier Claude model release, examining what the disclosed capability evaluations and safety testing reveal about the model's behavior and risk profile. This matters because system cards remain one of the few public windows into frontier model risk assessments, and expert scrutiny of their contents helps surface gaps between lab claims and independent verification.

3. **[AI #194: Spy Versus Guy](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-194-spy-versus-guy)** — Zvi's latest weekly roundup covers the current state of play across labs, policy, and safety research ahead of "The Takeoff" conference in Washington DC, synthesizing a broad sweep of developments into a single digestible briefing. This matters as a high-signal aggregator that practitioners and policymakers use to stay current across a fast-moving field, shaping which stories get amplified into broader discourse.

4. **[A New Kind of Prediction Market for Science](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7xJeaAKTKxbHL6F9a/a-new-kind-of-prediction-market-for-science)** — This post proposes a decentralized prediction-market mechanism to help triage the exploding volume of AI-assisted research submissions, arguing that peer review can no longer keep pace with the combination of AI-generated "slop" and genuinely valuable work flooding arXiv. This matters for AI safety research specifically, where distinguishing rigorous alignment work from noise is increasingly difficult as AI tools accelerate paper production across the field.

## Policy & Governance

- **[AI Kill Switches: What Policymakers Should Know](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/ai-kill-switches-what-policymakers-should-know/)** (Georgetown CSET) — This piece unpacks the technical reality behind proposals requiring "AI kill switches," breaking down what such mechanisms could look like at different layers of the AI stack (hardware, model, deployment infrastructure) and what tradeoffs policymakers need to understand before mandating them. This matters because kill-switch requirements are gaining traction in legislative proposals, yet their feasibility and failure modes are poorly understood outside technical circles, making this a timely resource for regulators drafting binding emergency-stop provisions.
