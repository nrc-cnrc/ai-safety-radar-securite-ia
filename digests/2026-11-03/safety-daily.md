# Daily Digest (2026-11-03)

## Top 3 Developments

**IAPS Memo Targets Physical Security Gaps in U.S. Chip Export Controls** - [How to Secure Advanced U.S. Chips](https://www.iaps.ai/research/how-to-secure-advanced-us-chips) from the Institute for AI Policy and Strategy lays out concrete recommendations for hardening advanced AI chips against theft, smuggling, and diversion. This matters because compute governance regimes are only as strong as the physical and supply-chain security underpinning them, and this addresses a largely unaddressed implementation gap in export-control policy.

**Anthropic Advances Cryptographic Verification of Model Inference** - [Anthropic's research on provable inference](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference) uses zkSNARKs to verify that a claimed model inference was actually run, without revealing weights or proprietary details. This matters because cryptographically verifiable inference could become foundational infrastructure for third-party audits and compute-governance enforcement that doesn't require labs to disclose IP.

**New Study Finds Predictive Safety Guardrails for Agents Don't Reliably Improve Safety** - [Predictive Safety Guardrails Do Not Always Make LLM Agents Safer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02815) systematically tests world-model-driven guardrails that anticipate action consequences before execution and finds end-to-end safety gains don't reliably follow from accurate prediction or faithful use of predicted risk. This matters because it is a direct corrective to a popular agent safety-engineering approach, echoing this week's broader pattern (e.g., [control-monitor gaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41713) on Nov 2) of safety evaluations and mechanisms producing illusory rather than genuine robustness.

## Risk Watch

**Increased Risks**
- Predictive world-model guardrails for LLM agents can give false confidence of safety without actually reducing harm ([Predictive Safety Guardrails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02815)).
- MCP tool poisoning is a broad, underappreciated supply-chain attack surface across agent frameworks, confirmed via real tool execution rather than model compliance alone ([MCP Tool Poisoning study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03555)).
- Standard context-compaction techniques for long-running agents can silently erode embedded safety rules ([ARCBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03223)).
- Advanced U.S. AI chips remain exposed to theft, smuggling, and diversion due to physical/supply-chain security gaps ([IAPS memo](https://www.iaps.ai/research/how-to-secure-advanced-us-chips)).
- A widely-cited harm-evaluation benchmark's scoring was silently invalidated by an unpinned dependency update ([HarmBench issue](https://github.com/centerforaisafety/HarmBench/issues/102)).

**Decreased Risks**
- Cryptographic "provable inference" could enable third-party verification of model behavior without weight disclosure, supporting future audit and compute-governance regimes ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference)).
- Interpretability probes can detect collusive intent among agents before it manifests in outputs, offering a proactive intervention point ([Multi-Agent Collusion and the J-Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02153)).
- OWASP's Agent Memory Guard project advanced in maturity with new approval gates and ML-based injection detection for persistent agent memory ([OWASP promotion](https://github.com/OWASP/www-project-agent-memory-guard/pull/201)).
- Faster, more memory-efficient training-data attribution tooling improves the practicality of auditing what shaped model behavior ([bergson release](https://github.com/EleutherAI/bergson/releases/tag/v2.9.1)).
