# Blogs & News (2026-10-31)

## Top Stories

**[Was I wrong about open models & cyber risks?](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/was-i-wrong-about-open-models-and)** — Nathan Lambert revisits his prior skepticism about AI-enabled cyber risk in light of "Black October," a wave of critical vulnerability disclosures (including PHP2Shell and GitLab issues) that he argues signals the point where AI-assisted exploit discovery and weaponization have begun materializing in the wild rather than remaining theoretical. He walks through what changed in his threat model and what evidence would change it further. This matters because it's a rare instance of a prominent open-model advocate publicly updating toward taking AI-driven cyber risk more seriously, which could shift the broader debate on open-weight release policy.

**[AI #192: Anthropic Is Going Public](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-192-anthropic-is-going-public)** — Zvi Mowshowitz covers Anthropic's S-1 filing ahead of its IPO, alongside the week's broader AI news roundup. The move to public markets raises fresh questions about how commercial and shareholder pressures will interact with Anthropic's safety-first mission and governance structure (e.g., its Long-Term Benefit Trust) once subject to public-market scrutiny and quarterly performance incentives. This matters because it's a pivotal governance test case for whether a frontier lab explicitly built around AI-safety commitments can maintain them under public-market pressure.

**[Microdosing the spirit of Anthropic](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/oL7aFoRmRXGwEMhCm/microdosing-the-spirit-of-anthropic)** — A LessWrong essay develops a "microdosing" metaphor for the epistemic environment inside AI labs: not that staff hold extreme beliefs, but that constant internal debate plus a portfolio of individually-reasonable positions converges, in aggregate, toward conclusions that happen to serve organizational interests. The piece explores epistemic advantages (proximity to frontier capabilities, rich internal discourse) and disadvantages (subtle motivated reasoning, insularity) of being embedded inside a lab. This matters because it offers a useful conceptual lens for evaluating the reliability of safety claims and risk assessments coming from inside frontier labs, relevant to both external auditors and policymakers relying on lab self-reports.

**[Reviewing my 2017 AI Risk Project](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/s4z629mkjFtQCY654/reviewing-my-2017-ai-risk-project)** — A retrospective on an early (2017) personal investigation into whether AI risk was a credible, tractable cause area, written at a time when the author's ML-researcher friends were broadly dismissive of the concern despite EA orgs like 80,000 Hours ranking it as a top priority. The post reflects on what the author got right or wrong about tractability, timelines, and community consensus with the benefit of hindsight. This matters as a useful calibration check on how community forecasts and expert disagreement about AI risk have evolved over nearly a decade, informative for anyone assessing the track record of AI-safety forecasting.
