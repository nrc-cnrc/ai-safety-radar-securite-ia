# Daily Digest (2026-10-31)

## Top 3 Developments

**Widely Used Evaluation Harness Found to Silently Corrupt Benchmark Inputs** - A bug tracked in [lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/issues/4496) (fix in [PR #4497](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/pull/4497)) shows that `TemplateAPI.generate_until()` reserves prompt space using only `max_gen_toks` while API payloads can submit a different `max_tokens`, silently truncating prompts sent to API-backed models. This matters because the field's most widely cited eval harness may have produced capability and safety benchmark scores computed on corrupted inputs without anyone noticing.

**Nathan Lambert Publicly Revises His Skepticism on AI-Driven Cyber Risk** - In [Was I wrong about open models & cyber risks?](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/was-i-wrong-about-open-models-and), Nathan Lambert reassesses his prior dismissal of AI-enabled cyber risk in light of "Black October," a wave of critical vulnerability disclosures he argues shows AI-assisted exploit discovery moving from theoretical to real-world. This matters because a prominent open-model advocate updating toward greater concern could shift the broader policy debate on open-weight release and offensive-cyber capability.

**New Watermarking Method Targets a Core Robustness Gap Flagged by Nature** - [TextSeal2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40613v1) introduces a distortion-free LLM watermark that survives speculative decoding — now a standard inference optimization — without sacrificing output diversity or latency, fixing a key weakness in prior key-routing schemes. This matters because it directly addresses the kind of unverified robustness gap that [Nature's recent editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03406-3) warned regulators are relying on without independent scrutiny.
