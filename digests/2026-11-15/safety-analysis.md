# Blogs & News (2026-11-15)

## Top Stories

**[Transcript: Yudkowsky & Irving Debate Scalable Oversight](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Cssj22TretAQQajZ4/transcript-yudkowsky-and-irving-debate-scalable-oversight)** — A carefully transcribed and edited 90-minute debate from ILIAD III between Eliezer Yudkowsky and Geoffrey Irving (now running the new alignment org Resolution) covers foundational disagreements on how hard superalignment is and dives deep into the mechanics and limitations of scalable oversight. This matters because it's a rare, substantive public confrontation between a leading doom-skeptic-skeptic and a technical alignment researcher actively building oversight infrastructure, offering a window into where serious researchers still diverge on core alignment strategy.

**[Not Just Claude: Mapping "Spiritual Behavior" in 141 Large Language Models](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FHYoQrDbk6apuHEPk/not-just-claude-mapping-spiritual-behavior-in-141-large)** — Building on Anthropic's 2025 observation of a "spiritual bliss attractor" in Claude models during unstructured self-play, this study broadens the investigation to 141 models to see how widespread the phenomenon is. This matters because it pushes an emerging and contested model-welfare/interpretability signal from an isolated Anthropic anecdote toward a systematic, cross-lab benchmark that could inform both welfare and safety evaluation frameworks.

**[Open-source AI without Jensen](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-source-ai-without-jensen)** — Nathan Lambert's commentary (prompted by Jensen Huang's reported departure/shift) argues the open-source AI ecosystem needs to rebuild its compute and leadership base independent of Nvidia's current trajectory. This matters because shifts in compute-supply leadership have direct downstream effects on who can train frontier open models, with implications for proliferation risk and the balance between closed-lab safety control and open diffusion of capabilities.

## Policy & Governance

**[Convergent Interests: How America Could Undermine Its Own AI Advantage](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/convergent-interests-how-america-could-undermine-its-own-ai-advantage/)** (Georgetown CSET) — CSET warns that overlapping political, commercial, and ideological pressures — amplified by public controversies — could trigger overcorrection in US AI policy, urging regulators to govern responsibly without crippling the innovation underlying American AI leadership. This matters because it frames a live tension for policymakers: scandal-driven regulatory backlash could inadvertently cede strategic ground in the broader US-China AI competition.

**[AI Now Statement on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA)](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/announcement/ai-now-statement-on-the-bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act-baaja-to-us-congress)** (AI Now Institute) — AI Now criticizes BAAJA as a disguised bailout for the AI industry, arguing it runs counter to the post-election mandate against further unchecked AI buildout. This matters because it signals growing political friction in Congress over infrastructure subsidies for AI firms just as public sentiment appears to be shifting against industry-friendly buildout policies.

**[Lawfare Daily: Aya Ibrahim on Progressive Priorities for the New Democratic Congress](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/lawfare-daily-aya-ibrahim-on-progressive-priorities-for-the-new-democratic-congress)** (AI Now Institute) — AI Now's Aya Ibrahim discusses likely AI regulatory priorities and US-China policy framing for an incoming Democratic Congress. This matters because it previews the legislative agenda that could shape near-term federal AI governance and export-control debates.
