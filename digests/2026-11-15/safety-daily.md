# Daily Digest (2026-11-15)

## Top 3 Developments

**CSET Warns That Scandal-Driven Backlash Could Undermine US AI Leadership** - [Convergent Interests: How America Could Undermine Its Own AI Advantage](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/convergent-interests-how-america-could-undermine-its-own-ai-advantage/) argues that overlapping political, commercial, and ideological pressures amplified by recent controversies risk triggering regulatory overcorrection that cedes strategic ground to China. This matters because it frames a concrete tension policymakers now face: punitive responses to scandal could do more lasting damage to US AI capacity than the incidents themselves.

**New Interpretability Method Localizes Goal Representations Inside LLMs** - [Goal Encoding in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17297) identifies a sparse set of attention heads that encode a model's upcoming sub-goal in a multi-subtask coding setup, and shows these representations can predict actions before they're generated. This matters because detecting internal goal-directed planning is directly relevant to catching deceptive alignment and mesa-optimization before they manifest behaviorally.

**Study Shows Image Resizing Can Expose or Hide Adversarial Instructions in VLM Pipelines** - [Scale-Induced Semantic Shift in Visual Prompt Injection Against VLMs and Web Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16744) demonstrates that routine image compression/resizing in deployment pipelines can reveal or conceal injected adversarial instructions depending on resolution. This matters because it shows static prompt-injection benchmarks substantially underestimate real attack surfaces once ordinary preprocessing steps are accounted for.
