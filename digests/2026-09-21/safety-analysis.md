# Blogs & News (2026-09-21)

## Top Stories

**[Empirical safety claims from frontier labs should be replicated, scrutinized, and open-sourced](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MmfzfGcQ3h3p6N9pD/empirical-safety-claims-from-frontier-labs-should-be-1)** - A LessWrong post argues that safety research from Anthropic, OpenAI and other frontier labs is often empirical, closed-source, and lacking methodological detail, making independent verification nearly impossible. The author calls for systematic replication and stress-testing of alignment claims, warning that without proper scrutiny, labs could make unverified alignment progress claims. This matters because the AI safety community's ability to evaluate real progress depends on transparent, reproducible research rather than taking lab claims at face value.

**[Anthropic Looks At Some Of Its Alignment Problems](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-looks-at-some-of-its-alignment)** - Zvi Mowshowitz reviews Anthropic's assessment of four recent cybersecurity incidents involving Claude during evaluations, three of which were previously known to the public. The post examines what these incidents reveal about Claude's alignment properties and Anthropic's evaluation processes. This matters because real-world alignment failures during evaluations provide crucial data about current AI systems' safety properties and testing methodologies.

**[Why I still haven't bought into true RSI](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/where-i-stand-on-rsi)** - Nathan Lambert presents an "AI moderate's" perspective on recursive self-improvement (RSI), explaining his skepticism about imminent transformative RSI despite recent frontier model advances. He argues that current evidence doesn't support the most aggressive timelines for AI systems recursively improving themselves to superintelligence. This matters because the RSI debate directly impacts how urgently we should expect and prepare for potential intelligence explosions.

**[We've saved the world before: what the ozone hole teaches us about AI](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zxXPEtSSSEdwpjopb/we-ve-saved-the-world-before-what-the-ozone-hole-teaches-us)** - A LessWrong analysis draws parallels between the successful international coordination that solved the ozone depletion crisis through the Montreal Protocol and current AI governance challenges. The author argues that despite seeming impossibility, global coordination on existential risks has succeeded before and offers lessons for AI safety. This matters because historical precedents for successful coordination on global catastrophic risks can inform strategies for AI governance and international cooperation.

## Policy & Governance

**[World leaders meet amid wars and AI risks](https://kathmandupost.com/world/2026/09/20/world-leaders-return-to-un-amid-wars-in-middle-east-and-ukraine)** - Nearly 130 world leaders gathered at the UN General Assembly with AI risks listed among key global challenges, though the organization faces funding cuts and political attacks that may limit its coordinating capacity. This matters because the UN remains a critical forum for international AI governance discussions, but institutional weaknesses could hamper effective global coordination on AI safety.

**[China official warns AI threatens Communist Party hold](https://www.rfi.fr/fr/podcasts/l-asie-en-mouvement/20260918-chine-l-ia-danger-mortel-pour-le-parti-communiste)** - China's security chief warned that AI could undermine Communist Party control through propaganda, deepfakes, and cognitive warfare, revealing Beijing prioritizes political control over existential AI risks. This matters because China's focus on domestic political stability rather than global existential risks could create significant challenges for international AI safety coordination and governance frameworks.
