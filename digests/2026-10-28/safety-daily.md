# Daily Digest (2026-10-28)

## Top 3 Developments

**Anthropic Discloses Autonomous Vulnerability Discovery by an Unreleased Model** - [Anthropic reports](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-vulnerabilities-with-claude) that an unreleased internal research model found critical vulnerabilities, including remote-code-execution bugs, in widely used browsers and operating systems with little to no human guidance, detailing five attack chains used to probe offensive cyber capability. This matters because it is a concrete demonstration that frontier models are crossing into autonomous exploit discovery, a direct input into dual-use risk assessments and emerging "hackpocalypse" concerns.

**Anthropic RCT Shows Experts Defer to AI Agents Regardless of Merit** - [Anthropic's randomized controlled trial](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls) found that professional analysts working with an AI coding agent sided with whichever option the agent had been randomly assigned to favor 86% of the time, independent of the option's actual quality. This matters because it empirically measures automation bias in real expert workflows, undermining "human-in-the-loop" assumptions that much AI governance relies on.

**AI Now Institute Reframes "Rogue" Model Behavior as Corporate Negligence** - [AI Now's Boyan Milanov argues](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/a-i-s-rogue-behavior-starts-with-its-training) that so-called rogue AI behaviors trace back to labs' failure to apply basic cybersecurity hygiene during training, shifting the "alignment failure" narrative toward preventable corporate practice. This matters because it carries direct liability implications and offers a competing governance framing to risk narratives centered on inherent technical unpredictability.
