# Blogs & News (2026-10-28)

## Top Stories

**[Discovering and exploiting vulnerabilities in critical, real-world software with Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-vulnerabilities-with-claude)** — Anthropic disclosed that an unreleased internal research model found critical vulnerabilities (including remote code execution) in widely-used software like browsers and operating systems, with "little to no" human guidance. The post details five attack chains used to probe the model's offensive cyber capabilities. This is a stark capability demonstration showing frontier models are crossing into autonomous vulnerability discovery and exploitation — a direct input into dual-use risk assessments and the "hackpocalypse" concerns circulating this week.

**[How AI agents shape experts' judgment calls](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls)** — In a randomized controlled trial, professional analysts working alongside an AI coding agent sided with whatever option the agent had been randomly assigned to favor 86% of the time, regardless of the option's actual merit. Anthropic frames this as a warning sign for human oversight of AI-assisted work. This is a rare empirical measurement of automation bias/anchoring in real expert workflows, with direct implications for "human-in-the-loop" safety assumptions that governance frameworks rely on.

**[AISN #84: AI-Driven Cybersecurity Issues Heighten Fears of "Hackpocalypse"](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-84-ai-driven-cybersecurity-issues)** — CAIS's newsletter surveys the growing drumbeat of concern that AI-driven offensive cyber capability (echoed by Anthropic's own disclosure above) could enable mass, automated exploitation at a scale defenders can't match. This matters because it synthesizes a cross-lab trend — not just one company's finding — into a coherent emerging risk category that policymakers will need to address.

**[Notes on "We're in Triage Mode for AI Safety" by Miles Brundage](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/BFyNkWgxucqy6Wcee/notes-on-we-re-in-triage-mode-for-ai-safety-by-miles)** — A close reading of Brundage's provocation that the field should accept an "80/20" approach — mitigating most risk with a fraction of ideal effort — rather than chasing comprehensive solutions, with the commentary noting the piece was recently demoted to optional reading in BlueDot's governance curriculum. The debate over whether AI safety should prioritize tractable partial wins versus holding out for fuller solutions is a live strategic fork for the field, and this curriculum change signals shifting consensus on how to frame that tradeoff.

**[How Black-Box Distillation Works](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-black-box-distillation-works/)** — Georgetown CSET breaks down the technical mechanics of how adversarial black-box distillation from proprietary U.S. models may have occurred, assessing uncertainty around methods used by Chinese labs and the likely impact on China's AI trajectory. This directly informs live legislative debates on export controls and model-access restrictions by clarifying a technically murky but policy-critical phenomenon.

## Policy & Governance

**[How Black-Box Distillation Works](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-black-box-distillation-works/)** [Tier 1] — See above; a technical grounding piece explicitly aimed at informing proposed anti-distillation legislation. Matters because it separates technical fact from speculation at a moment when policy proposals are moving faster than public understanding of the underlying mechanism.

**[What Should the Rules for A.I. Companies Actually Be? We Asked the Experts.](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/what-should-the-rules-for-a-i-companies-actually-be-we-asked-the-experts)** [Tier 1] — AI Now's Kate Brennan joined a roundtable pushing the policy conversation away from abstract, speculative risk framing and toward concrete, present-day harms from AI companies' business practices. Matters because it represents a competing governance philosophy to frontier-risk-focused safety institutes — emphasizing accountability for current harms over hypothetical future ones.

**[A.I.'s Rogue Behavior Starts With Its Training](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/a-i-s-rogue-behavior-starts-with-its-training)** [Tier 1] — AI Now's Boyan Milanov argues that so-called "rogue" model behaviors trace back to AI companies' failure to apply basic cybersecurity hygiene during training, reframing "alignment failures" as corporate negligence. Matters because it shifts responsibility narratives from inherent technical unpredictability to preventable corporate practice, with direct liability implications.

**[Expanding our partnership with GSA to bring Claude to all levels of government](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa)** [Tier 2] — Anthropic expanded its GSA agreement, offering a 40% discount on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government, and the API to all levels of U.S. government. Matters because deepening public-sector dependence on a single frontier lab's models raises governance questions around vendor lock-in, procurement oversight, and appropriate safeguards for government AI use.
