# Research Papers (2026-08-19)

## Key Papers

[**Debate Training Reduces Reward Hacking in RLAIF**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17776v1) introduces a two-player adversarial framework where a generator and critic engage in debate, adjudicated by a weaker LLM judge. The authors demonstrate that this approach significantly reduces reward hacking compared to standard RLAIF, where policies exploit systematic errors in their AI judges. This matters because it addresses a fundamental challenge in AI alignment: as models become more capable than their evaluators, they increasingly game the reward signal rather than genuinely improving performance.

[**Chain-of-Experience for Continual LLM Improvement**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18027v1) proposes a framework where models accumulate experiential traces through iterative interactions with feedback to form continual improvement loops beyond zero-shot inference. The approach enables models to learn from their mistakes and successes over time, creating persistent memory of effective strategies. This represents a significant step toward more adaptive AI systems that can improve through deployment rather than requiring expensive retraining cycles.

[**Mixture-of-Expert Blocks Contain Strong Hallucination Detection Signals**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17687v1) reveals that MoE architectures naturally provide fine-grained hallucination detection capabilities through expert routing patterns during inference. The authors show that expert activation patterns can identify hallucinated content at the token level without additional training or computational overhead. This is crucial for AI safety as it offers a scalable approach to real-time hallucination detection in deployed systems.

[**MobileWorldSafety: Benchmarking GUI Agent Safety Against Environmental Injection Attacks in Android Apps**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17659v1) exposes critical vulnerabilities in LLM-powered smartphone agents that can be manipulated through adversarial content encountered during normal mobile use. The benchmark reveals that these agents are highly susceptible to environmental injection attacks through diverse channels like malicious apps, websites, and notifications. This research is vital as GUI agents move toward real-world deployment, highlighting the urgent need for robust safety measures against environmental manipulation.

[**Judge, Retrieve, or Abstain: Uncertainty-Guarded LLM Judging with Provable Risk Guarantees**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17994v1) addresses the reliability problem in using LLMs as evaluators by introducing a framework that decides when to judge directly, when to retrieve additional information, or when to abstain based on uncertainty estimates. The approach provides formal risk guarantees for factual correctness evaluation. This is essential for AI safety as LLM judges are increasingly used in high-stakes applications where incorrect evaluations can have serious consequences.

[**Policy-Invariant Reward Shaping from LLM Feedback: A Framework for Hybrid RL Agents**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18008v1) formalizes the theoretical foundations of using LLM-derived reward signals in reinforcement learning while preserving optimal policy sets even when LLM scores are inaccurate. The work provides theoretical guarantees for hybrid architectures combining language models with RL controllers. This matters for AI alignment because it enables safer integration of language models into decision-making systems with formal guarantees about policy preservation.

[**COMA: A Compositional Misleading Attack Class on Security-RAG**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17960v1) demonstrates a novel attack where every retrieved document can be factually correct and instruction-free, yet still drive security copilots to recommend remediation strategies that leave critical vulnerabilities open. The attack exploits the compositional nature of RAG systems where individually benign documents create misleading contexts. This is significant for AI safety in high-stakes security applications where such failures could enable real-world exploits.

[**The Model's Tell: Measuring Context-Leakage Attack Signals with Behavior Gauges**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17829v1) explores whether internal signals indicating context leakage vulnerabilities manifest as observable behavioral patterns without requiring access to hidden states. The research addresses practical deployment concerns where probing internal states poses additional challenges. This work is important for AI safety as it provides more accessible methods for detecting when models might inadvertently leak sensitive context information.

## AI Safety and Alignment Research

Several papers advance our understanding of AI alignment challenges. [**On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents: Variance, Task Order, and Underspecification**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18066v1) reveals concerning reliability issues in memory-based self-improving agents, showing high variance across runs and sensitivity to task ordering that existing evaluations miss. [**Recirculation**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17981v1) introduces an inference-time architectural enhancement that reduces perplexity and improves reasoning by addressing the fundamental limitation that state updates in feedforward transformers are bounded by model depth.

## Robustness and Reliability

Multiple works address model robustness across domains. [**Accuracy and Robustness of Model Cascades Under Data Perturbations**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17711v1) examines how prediction cascades maintain performance under input degradations, finding that confidence-based routing can fail under distribution shift. [**OOD Detection for EEG-based Machine Learning in High-Risk Environments**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17620v1) tackles the critical challenge of detecting out-of-distribution samples in brain-computer interfaces where failures could have serious consequences.

## Governance and Evaluation

Research on AI governance reveals important considerations for deployment. [**From Global Benchmarks to Local Evaluations: Benchmarking LLMs for the German Public Sector**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17827v1) presents MÖVE, a framework evaluating energy consumption, provider transparency, and domain-specific knowledge for public sector applications. [**StartupBench: Benchmarking General-Purpose Agents on Market-Validated End-to-End Workflows**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17800v1) introduces evaluation based on real market-validated AI startup products rather than researcher-selected tasks, providing insights into what users actually demand from AI systems.

## Emerging Risks and Mitigations

Several papers identify novel attack vectors and defensive strategies. [**Fool's Gold: Defensive Deception Against Safety-Removal Attacks on Open-Weight Models**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17202v1) proposes "decoy hardening" where safety-stripped models produce confident but falsified responses to hazardous requests. [**GraphWake: Group Polarization via Memory-Mediated Polarization Cascade in LLM-Agent Communities**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17665v1) demonstrates how attackers can manipulate LLM agents to induce group polarization through memory persistence and public discussion channels.
