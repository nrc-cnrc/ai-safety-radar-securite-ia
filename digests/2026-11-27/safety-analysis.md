# Blogs & News (2026-11-27)

## Top Stories

- **[Claude and Levent Alpöge Prove the Riemann Hypothesis](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-and-levent-alpoge-prove-the)** — Zvi Mowshowitz covers a startling claim: a collaboration between Anthropic's Claude and mathematician Levent Alpöge reportedly produced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, long considered the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics. Whether or not the proof fully holds up to peer scrutiny, the claim itself signals a potential inflection point in AI-assisted mathematical research. **Why it matters**: If substantiated, this would be one of the clearest demonstrations yet that frontier models can contribute original, high-level mathematical reasoning rather than just synthesizing known results — with major implications for how we forecast AI R&D acceleration and "capability surprise" more broadly.

- **[AI #196: The Return](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-196-the-return)** — Zvi's latest weekly roundup covers the ongoing churn of model releases, policy developments, and safety debates, framed through a Thanksgiving lens of gratitude for the community's work. **Why it matters**: As the single most comprehensive weekly synthesis in the AI safety commentary space, this issue serves as a useful index of what the community is tracking and prioritizing right now.

- **[I don't expect superposition in transformer MLP neurons](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/o9dPGxEbpQrnasgDB/i-don-t-expect-superposition-in-transformer-mlp-neurons)** — A technical interpretability post arguing that superposition-style feature compression likely occurs in the residual stream of transformers but not within MLP neurons themselves, pushing back on common assumptions in mechanistic interpretability work. **Why it matters**: Correctly locating where superposition occurs is foundational for interpretability research aimed at reverse-engineering model internals — getting this wrong could misdirect efforts to build trustworthy, inspectable models.

- **[You (yes You) might secretly be really good at xrisk advocacy!](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/REeaaC9hzrsxw7jx6/you-yes-you-might-secretly-be-really-good-at-xrisk-advocacy)** — A retrospective from a former Senior Safety Scientist in Yoshua Bengio's group, now leading ControlAI's Canada efforts, reflecting on a year of grassroots x-risk advocacy and encouraging more community members to get involved in policy outreach. **Why it matters**: It highlights the growing professionalization of AI safety advocacy as a distinct track from technical research, and signals ControlAI's expanding footprint in shaping national-level AI policy conversations.

## Policy & Governance

- **[IAPS Policy Memo Series: Securing the Frontier](https://www.iaps.ai/research/scaling-securely-recommendations-for-the-executive-branch)** — The Institute for AI Policy and Strategy released a coordinated package of six memos aimed at the U.S. executive branch, covering: [scaling AI securely](https://www.iaps.ai/research/scaling-securely-recommendations-for-the-executive-branch), [urgent action to secure frontier capabilities](https://www.iaps.ai/research/advancing-ai-capabilities-call-for-urgent-action-to-secure-the-frontier), [oversight of high-risk AI development](https://www.iaps.ai/research/ensure-oversight-of-high-risk-ai-development), [control of high-risk AI deployment](https://www.iaps.ai/research/secure-and-control-high-risk-ai-deployment), [preserving U.S. leadership in frontier AI](https://www.iaps.ai/research/preserve-us-leadership-in-frontier-ai), [building resilience against AI-enabled threats](https://www.iaps.ai/research/build-resilience-against-ai-enabled-threats), and [monitoring China's frontier AI risks](https://www.iaps.ai/research/monitor-and-manage-chinas-frontier-ai-risks). The memos collectively argue that frontier labs are now using unreleased internal models to accelerate their own R&D, and call for new federal institutions capable of overseeing and responding to capability advances at a matching tempo. **Why it matters**: This is a substantial, coordinated policy push timed to influence executive branch AI strategy, explicitly framing internal model use at frontier labs as a governance blind spot requiring urgent institutional response — a notable escalation in how think tanks are approaching U.S. AI security policy.
