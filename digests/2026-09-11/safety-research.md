# Research Papers (2026-09-11)

## Key Papers

### [The Last AI Built by Humans: Toward Genuine Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11873v1)
This extensive work with 32 co-authors introduces a roadmap for recursive self-improvement (RSI) in AI systems, progressing through four autonomy stages: improvement-execution, improvement-strategy, experience-acquisition, and environment-adaptation. The authors use the Headroom-Closed Index to identify limitations in current LLMs and outline pathways to systems that can genuinely improve themselves recursively. This represents a significant theoretical framework for understanding the next phase of AI capability development and the potential for truly autonomous AI systems.

### [ActSafeGuard: Differentiable and Training-Aligned Constraint Enforcement for Flow-Matching Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11697v1)
Addresses a critical safety gap in Vision-Language-Action models where generated actions may violate physical constraints. ActSafeGuard provides differentiable constraint enforcement during training rather than just post-hoc correction, ensuring safety guarantees for robotic manipulation tasks. The method demonstrates how to maintain safety properties while preserving policy performance, which is essential for reliable deployment of AI systems in physical environments.

### [From Protocols to Evidence: Bounded Claims for AI in Service of the Common Good](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11910v1)
This governance-focused paper argues that AI deployment reveals and can either repair or compound institutional failures in responsiveness, belonging, care, and accountability. The authors propose moving beyond principles to protocols with bounded claims about AI system behavior. This work provides important frameworks for evaluating AI systems not just on technical metrics but on their broader institutional and social impacts.

### [SpecGuard: Inference-Time Backdoor Detection For Free](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11799v1)
Presents a practical solution for detecting backdoors in large language models during inference without computational overhead. The method uses speculative decoding properties to identify when models exhibit anomalous behavior patterns that indicate backdoor activation. Given the prevalence of third-party model deployment and fine-tuning, this approach addresses a critical security vulnerability in deployed AI systems.

### [Artificial Id: Drive and Persistent Alignment in Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11911v1)
Proposes an "artificial id" as an adaptive internal drive system for agentic AI that determines when to continue, stop, or change behavior without external specification. The work addresses fundamental control problems as AI systems become more autonomous and persistent across task boundaries. This represents important progress toward understanding how to maintain alignment in systems that operate with greater independence.

### [BlueSTAR: Tiered Agentic Architecture for Autonomous Cyber Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11852v1)
Develops a practical architecture for autonomous cyber defense using large language models in a tiered system that processes security telemetry and responds to threats. The system addresses the speed mismatch between automated attacks and human response times by providing automated correlation and response capabilities. This demonstrates how AI safety techniques can be applied to critical infrastructure protection.

### [Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11699v1)
Challenges conventional on-policy self-distillation by showing it can degrade reasoning performance by suppressing exploratory reasoning paths. The proposed negative self-distillation method explicitly teaches models to avoid flawed reasoning patterns while preserving beneficial exploration. This work reveals subtle failure modes in current alignment techniques and provides improved methods for training reasoning capabilities.

### [Trustworthy AI beyond the technical: a three-layer framework informed by Chinese-language scholarship](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03366-2)
This peer-reviewed journal article addresses a significant gap in AI governance literature by incorporating Chinese-language scholarship on trustworthy AI. The authors develop a three-layer framework based on analysis of 43 CSSCI-indexed articles, providing cross-cultural perspectives on AI trustworthiness that are often missing from Western-dominated discourse. This work is particularly important for developing globally inclusive approaches to AI safety and governance.

### [MindTopo: Can Foundation Models Reason in Topological Space?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11900v1)
Introduces a benchmark for evaluating foundation models' understanding of topological concepts like continuity, connectedness, and compactness that are fundamental to spatial reasoning. The work reveals limitations in current models' grasp of topological invariants, which are crucial for robust spatial understanding in applications ranging from robotics to mathematical reasoning.

### [Who Bears the Risk When Generative AI Enters Transport? A Distributional Sociotechnical Audit of Algorithmic Equity, Synthetic-Data Validity, and Public Trust](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11611v1)
Develops a comprehensive framework for auditing distributional risks when AI systems are deployed in transportation applications. The Distributional Sociotechnical Audit integrates algorithmic equity, data validity, and public trust measures, providing tools for measuring how risks are distributed across different populations. This work demonstrates how AI safety evaluation must consider broader social impacts beyond technical performance metrics.
