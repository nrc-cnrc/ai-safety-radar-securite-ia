# Blogs & News (2026-09-27)

## Top Stories

**[Claude Opus 5.5 Should Raise Your Ambitions](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-opus-55-should-raise-your)** - Zvi Mowshowitz argues that Claude Opus 5.5, following GPT-6 Astra and Fable 5.1, represents another significant capability jump that should fundamentally change how we think about what's possible with AI assistance. He emphasizes that the model "just works" and "persists" in completing complex tasks, with universally positive user feedback. This matters because it signals we may be experiencing more rapid capability advances than expected, with implications for both AI safety timelines and economic disruption.

**[Evidence about risk should be transparent](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/evidence-about-risk-should-be-transparent)** - Ajeya Cotra and Kelsey Piper argue that developing effective AI safety standards requires transparent evidence about risks rather than relying on opaque expert judgment alone. They contend that without clear, verifiable evidence of risk factors, it becomes impossible to build consensus around appropriate safety measures or regulatory frameworks. This matters because transparency in risk assessment is crucial for building public trust and enabling effective governance as AI capabilities advance.

**[On Ezra Klein's Podcast With Jensen Huang](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-ezra-kleins-podcast-with-jensen)** - Zvi Mowshowitz analyzes Jensen Huang's recent podcast appearance, noting that the NVIDIA CEO "accidentally called for shutting down OpenAI and intentionally called for spending vastly more on safety." The analysis suggests Huang's comments reveal significant concerns about current AI development practices and safety investments. This matters because Huang's position at the center of AI hardware development gives his safety concerns particular weight in industry discussions.

**[Poverty in the midst of abundance: AI will make goods cheaper, but your labor will get cheaper faster](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eLXTcJfkheLbqZXHa/poverty-in-the-midst-of-abundance-ai-will-make-goods-cheaper)** - A LessWrong post argues that while AI will reduce the cost of goods, it will reduce the value of human labor even faster, potentially leading to widespread economic displacement despite technological abundance. The author uses energy costs as a proxy to illustrate how AI-equivalent labor is becoming dramatically cheaper than human work. This matters because it highlights potential economic risks from AI advancement that may require significant policy intervention to address.
