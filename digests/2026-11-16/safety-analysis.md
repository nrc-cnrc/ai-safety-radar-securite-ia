# Blogs & News (2026-11-16)

## Top Stories

**[The Siren Song of the Inside Game](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GSseMxyeJmqQgKQi4/the-siren-song-of-the-inside-game)** — This LessWrong essay argues that there's a structural bias pulling AI safety advocates toward "inside game" tactics (cultivating trust and access with frontier labs) at the expense of "outside game" accountability (public pressure, independent scrutiny). The author contends that incentives — social, financial, and reputational — systematically favor cozying up to labs over holding them accountable, and argues that now is a good moment to rebalance toward more external pressure. *This matters because it crystallizes a recurring tension in AI safety strategy: as labs increasingly fund, employ, or platform safety researchers, the field's capacity for independent oversight may be quietly eroding.*

**[The great human centipede that is AI Twitter](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Ctia6zyXF69N3tzhv/the-great-human-centipede-that-is-ai-twitter)** — Commentary dissecting the information cascade around a *New York Times* report alleging Jensen Huang had a "close relationship" with an individual U.S. officials believe was a Chinese intelligence asset. The piece traces how the story — sourced to unnamed officials "briefed on the surveillance" — propagated and mutated through AI Twitter, raising questions about leak motives, narrative framing, and the reliability of chained secondhand sourcing in fast-moving AI-adjacent news. *This matters because it illustrates how national-security narratives about chip/AI leadership figures can spread with weak sourcing, shaping policy and public perception of US-China AI competition before facts are verified.*

## Policy & Governance

**[Convergent Interests: How America Could Undermine Its Own AI Advantage](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/convergent-interests-how-america-could-undermine-its-own-ai-advantage/)** (Georgetown CSET) — This piece warns that political, commercial, and ideological pressures can converge around public controversies (scandals, safety incidents) to produce overcorrective regulation that undercuts U.S. AI leadership. CSET urges policymakers to pursue responsible governance without disabling the innovation base that sustains American competitiveness, cautioning against reactive policymaking driven by scandal-of-the-day dynamics. *This matters because it signals how think tanks are framing the coming policy debate: not whether to regulate AI, but how to avoid governance responses that are politically expedient but strategically self-defeating in the US-China AI race.*
