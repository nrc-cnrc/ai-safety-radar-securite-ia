# Blogs & News (2026-10-21)

## Top Stories

**[Review of the "Misalignment in High-Stakes Settings" Section in Anthropic's Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-20-misalignment-risk-report-august-2026-review/)** — METR published a detailed critique of the misalignment section in Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report, offering an external evaluator's perspective on how Anthropic frames and measures high-stakes misalignment risks. This matters because independent review of frontier labs' self-reported risk assessments is one of the few mechanisms available to stress-test internal safety claims before they shape public and regulatory trust.

**[A Brief(ish) Introduction to LM Psychology](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EW8oncpXdanfjssZ3/a-brief-ish-introduction-to-lm-psychology)** — This LessWrong piece argues that despite having complete mechanistic access to language models' computations, we still lack a predictive, generalizable framework for their behavior — motivating a new "LM psychology" research agenda distinct from pure interpretability. This matters because it crystallizes a conceptual gap in alignment research: knowing *how* a model computes something is not the same as being able to predict *when* it will generalize safely or unsafely.

**[Latest Open Artifacts (#25): DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, American-made models, and new regulatory controls](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-25-deepseek)** — Nathan Lambert surveys the fast-moving open-model landscape, highlighting new Chinese and US open releases alongside fresh export/regulatory controls from both governments. This matters because the open-model race is increasingly entangled with national compute and export-control policy, raising the stakes for how safety practices propagate (or fail to) across jurisdictions.

**[How I Discovered an Exoplanet & the Science Apocalypse](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kdybMpcFJ5iwCDJsd/how-i-discovered-an-exoplanet-and-the-science-apocalypse)** — A LessWrong contributor describes using Claude Opus 5.5 to identify exoplanet candidates and reflects on the near-term implications of agentic AI systems for the pace and structure of scientific discovery. This matters because concrete demonstrations of AI-driven scientific acceleration sharpen debates about dual-use capability growth outpacing societal and institutional adaptation.

## Policy & Governance

**[Anthropic Does Not Understand Why Its Draft Bill Was Unacceptable and Troubling](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qxAroimgs4BvBHz2i/anthropic-does-not-understand-why-its-draft-bill-was)** — Following the leak of Anthropic's draft federal AI legislation, the company's federal affairs lead publicly responded, but critics (via Zvi/LessWrong commentary) argue the response only addresses peripheral complaints while ignoring the core problem — the substance and process of circulating the bill itself. This matters because it signals a widening trust gap between a leading "safety-focused" lab and the AI safety community over how that lab engages in actual policymaking.

**[Cloud Compute: A Policy Escalation Framework](https://www.iaps.ai/research/cloud-compute-a-policy-escalation-framework)** — IAPS researchers Cassia King and Onni Aarne propose a tiered framework for escalating policy responses around cloud compute access, likely informing how governments might calibrate controls on compute as a governance lever. This matters because compute governance remains one of the most actionable and enforceable levers for AI safety policy, and structured escalation frameworks help bridge the gap between abstract compute-governance proposals and concrete regulatory design.

**[Latest Open Artifacts (#25): New regulatory controls in the US & China](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-25-deepseek)** — Beyond model releases, this piece notes fresh regulatory moves from both the US and Chinese governments affecting AI development and distribution. This matters because concurrent unilateral regulatory shifts in the two leading AI powers could reshape competitive dynamics and complicate international coordination on safety standards.
