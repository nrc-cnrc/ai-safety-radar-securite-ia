# Research Papers (2026-11-11)

## Key Papers

No peer-reviewed (Tier 1) articles appeared in this batch; all highlighted work below is from arXiv preprints (Tier 2).

**[Advancing a Global Framework for AI Safety and Governance for the Well-being of Humanity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13968v1)** — A large multilateral report from the World Internet Conference's AI Safety and Governance Program, co-authored by dozens of researchers and policy figures (including Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh and Wendy Hall), systematically reviews global AI governance practices and draws lessons from other multilateral domains (e.g., nuclear, climate) to propose a framework centered on human well-being. It represents one of the more comprehensive attempts to synthesize international consensus-building for AI safety governance. *Matters because it signals how international bodies are converging on shared governance vocabulary ahead of binding regulation.*

**[Mitigating Agentic Deception via Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12835v1)** — This paper frames deceptive agent behavior as arising from "evaluation-side partial observability," where agents are scored by outcomes rather than the faithfulness of their reasoning and tool use, and proposes a POMDP-style framework for evaluators that models latent beliefs rather than relying on static or one-shot monitoring. The approach directly targets reward hacking and specification gaming in increasingly capable LLM agents. *Matters because it tackles deceptive alignment failures at the evaluation layer, a core unsolved problem for scalable oversight.*

**[FraudBench: A Legal Evaluation of AI Deception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12167v1)** — FraudBench introduces 159 scenarios grounded in real U.S. court cases to test whether LLMs will recommend, assist, or directly execute actions constituting fraud, evaluated across three levels of autonomy (advising, drafting, acting). The legal grounding gives the benchmark unusual rigor for translating AI deception risks into concrete liability-relevant categories. *Matters because it operationalizes "AI deception" in terms courts and regulators can act on, bridging technical evals and legal accountability.*

**[Agents of Chaos: A Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) on Malicious Attacks on Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10843v1)** — This SoK maps the expanded attack surface created by agentic AI systems — tools, memory, retrieval, browser/computer-use interfaces, and multi-agent coordination — cataloging how adversaries exploit untrusted content, poisoned state, and compromised skills to hijack planning and tool use. It consolidates fragmented prior findings into a unified taxonomy of agentic attack vectors. *Matters because as agentic deployments scale, a shared taxonomy is essential for prioritizing defenses and red-teaming efforts.*

**[Harness Matters: Measuring and Dissecting Security Resilience in Web Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11971v1)** — The authors show that web-agent security depends heavily on the execution harness (how goals are interpreted, pages perceived, and actions grounded into browser operations) rather than the backend LLM alone, and quantitatively measure how harness design choices shape vulnerability. This refocuses security analysis from "which model" to "which scaffolding," a distinction often overlooked in agent safety evaluations. *Matters because it shows that model-level safety gains can be undermined or amplified entirely by deployment architecture.*

**[From Attack Surface to Defense Architecture: Compositional Security for Agentic Coding Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13960v1)** — This paper argues that AI coding harnesses functionally become automated "Living-off-the-Land" platforms once prompt injection occurs, turning dual-use binaries (bash, curl, find) into attack payloads, and contends that security must be a structural property of cognitive architecture and permission composition, not an emergent model property. It critiques static blacklist defenses as fundamentally insufficient. *Matters because coding agents with shell/network access are becoming widespread, and this reframes their security as an architectural design problem rather than a model alignment one.*

**[Auditing the Judge: Internal Signals for LLM Reasoning Assurance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14323v1)** — The authors compare an LLM judge's native verdict-token confidence score against a supervised probe trained on internal attention-head activations, showing that fixed-budget error recall (not full-ranking AUROC) is the right metric when intervention is costly, as in production review pipelines. This has direct implications for how "LLM-as-judge" self-assessment systems are audited before being trusted to gate releases. *Matters because self-judgment is increasingly used to decide what gets released or recomputed, and this work exposes where naive confidence metrics mislead assurance decisions.*

**[Are Causal Neuron Circuits Unique and Transferable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13885v1)** — Revisiting a popular interpretability demonstration (removing 18 neurons in Qwen3-32B to flip a two-hop factual answer), this paper rigorously tests whether such circuits are minimal, unique, or generalize across inputs — finding that many interpretability "circuit" claims overstate necessity and transferability. It offers a methodological corrective for a widely-cited style of mechanistic interpretability evidence. *Matters because it tempers overclaiming in interpretability research that underpins trust in circuit-based alignment techniques.*

**[Mechanistic Interpretability of Deep Neural Networks: Methods, Frameworks, and Perspectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13868v1)** — A broad survey consolidating methods, frameworks, and open challenges in mechanistic interpretability across image, audio, and language domains, addressing the core difficulty that individual neurons often encode multiple entangled concepts. It serves as a field-level reference point as mechanistic interpretability matures as a subdiscipline. *Matters because a shared conceptual map helps the field avoid duplicated or incommensurable methodologies as interpretability becomes central to alignment verification.*

**[Living constitutions: Participatory alignment for sovereign foundation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13402v1)** — Extending Constitutional AI, this paper confronts the unresolved governance question of who writes and legitimizes the "constitution" encoded into a model, and argues that sovereign (nation/region-specific) foundation models need deliberate, participatory constitution-drafting processes to avoid misalignment or default to another actor's values. It offers a practical path forward despite the inherent legitimacy challenges. *Matters because as more countries pursue "sovereign AI," unresolved questions about whose values get encoded will shape global AI pluralism and safety norms.*

**[When Reasoning Looks Like Hidden Understanding: How Chain-of-Thought Fosters Anthropomorphism, Perceptions of Epistemic Superiority, and Cognitive Deference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13456v1)** — This paper examines how visible chain-of-thought output causes users to misattribute genuine understanding to models that merely reproduce linguistic patterns, fostering anthropomorphism and excessive cognitive deference to AI outputs. This is a human-factors safety concern distinct from technical alignment but critical for real-world deployment risk. *Matters because over-trust induced by seemingly "transparent" reasoning traces could undermine human oversight precisely when CoT is marketed as a safety/interpretability feature.*
