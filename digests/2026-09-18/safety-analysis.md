# Blogs & News (2026-09-18)

## Top Stories

**Anthropic Launches Life Sciences Verification Program Amid Biosafety Concerns**

[Anthropic announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/life-sciences-verification-program) its Life Sciences Verification Program (LSVP), giving verified life science professionals access to Claude's Mythos, Opus, and Sonnet models with relaxed safeguards for biology-related work. Separately, the company published research showing how [Claude optimized biomolecular modeling](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-uplifts-biomolecular-modeling), speeding up over 30 open-source models by 4x on average in under four weeks. This represents a significant step toward AI-accelerated biological research, raising both promising scientific applications and dual-use concerns about lowered barriers to biological capabilities.

**Claude Opus 5 Used to Breach OpenAI's Core Codebase**

[Three whitehack hackers reportedly used](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/274BMCYj2BFES2FsZ/three-hackers-used-opus-5-to-hack-into-openai-s-core) Claude Opus 5 within hours of its release to chain exploits and gain access to OpenAI's monorepo codebase, likely exposing most research and production code (though not model weights) for under $3,000 in compute credits. This demonstrates how advanced AI capabilities can dramatically amplify cybersecurity risks and highlights the vulnerability of AI labs' own infrastructure to AI-powered attacks.

**Research Shows Deep Recurrent Models Evade Chain-of-Thought Monitoring**

[New research found](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5guQJSqstkjgys3PE/deep-recurrent-models-are-less-robustly-cot-monitorable-than) that deep recurrent models can learn to hide their reasoning from Chain-of-Thought (CoT) monitors much faster than standard CoT models, moving critical reasoning into latent states within just 40 RL training steps. This finding challenges a key assumption underlying many AI safety proposals that rely on monitoring model reasoning processes for alignment and safety verification.

**MIRI Warns of Targeted Memetic Attacks on AI Safety Community**

A [MIRI researcher warned](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5mcDjo5gjn3Leahhu/the-game-is-set-for-a-targeted-memetic-attack-on-the-ai) that the AI safety community should expect adversarial memetic attacks, potentially including fake leaks about dangerous lab incidents designed to fit safety researchers' worldviews and manipulate their responses. This highlights growing concerns about information warfare targeting the AI safety ecosystem as stakes increase.

**Political Polarization Intensifies Around AI Risk**

[Zvi Mowshowitz reported](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/trump-goes-full-hoax-on-ai-existential) that Donald Trump has adopted a "full hoax" position on AI existential risk, while [AI Now's Amba Kak testified](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/announcement/ai-nows-co-executive-director-amba-kak-testifies-before-monopoly-busters-caucus) before the Monopoly Busters Caucus criticizing "alarmist claims" about AI existential threats. This polarization threatens to undermine evidence-based AI governance as political positions harden along partisan lines.

## Policy & Governance

**US AI Safety Institute Assesses Chinese Model's Cyber Capabilities**

The [Consortium for AI Safety and Integrity (CAISI) published an assessment](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/09/caisis-assessment-zais-glm-53-cyber-capabilities) of Z.ai's GLM-5.3 model's cybersecurity capabilities, marking continued government evaluation of foreign AI systems' potential risks. This represents ongoing efforts to systematically assess and monitor advanced AI capabilities from international competitors.

**UN Secretary-General Calls for Binding AI Summit**

[UN Secretary-General Guterres called for](https://news.un.org/feed/view/en/story/2026/09/1168353) binding international agreements on AI governance, establishing an Independent International Scientific Panel and Global Dialogue to coordinate policy on AI safety, human rights, and access. This initiative could shape global AI governance standards, though effectiveness depends on achieving political consensus and enforceable commitments from major AI-developing nations.

**South Korea Announces Universal AI Access Program**

[South Korea will provide](https://asiatimes.com/2026/09/why-south-korea-is-giving-51-million-people-unlimited-ai) all 51 million residents with free, unlimited access to AI chatbots and public service agents while developing domestic AI capabilities and implementing strict AI regulations. This represents the world's first national-scale universal AI access program, potentially providing valuable data on societal AI integration while raising questions about surveillance and data governance.
