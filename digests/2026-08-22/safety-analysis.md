# Blogs & News (2026-08-22)

## Top Stories

**[AI Text Watermarking Is Free And Good](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-text-watermarking-is-free-and)** - Zvi Mowshowitz highlights Scott Aaronson's solution to AI text watermarking developed at OpenAI, which works by using a private source of pseudo-randomness for token selection that creates detectable patterns without affecting output quality. This matters because it offers a practical solution to AI-generated text detection that could help address misinformation and academic integrity concerns without imposing costs on model performance.

**[Evaluating Explanations of LLM Behavior In The Wild with Counterfactual Experiments](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ExB6KYDcznaFS72eT/evaluating-explanations-of-llm-behavior-in-the-wild-with)** - Researchers introduce CHIVE, an agentic pipeline that discovers unexpected LLM behaviors and tests explanations through counterfactual prompt edits, finding that current activation-reading interpretability tools provide no performance uplift over simply reading transcripts. This is significant because it challenges the effectiveness of current mechanistic interpretability approaches and suggests we may need fundamentally different tools to understand LLM behavior.

**[Misaligned AI in the Bronze Age](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/mPrbyBsGmNfWWgJmi/misaligned-ai-in-the-bronze-age)** - A creative historical analysis arguing that ancient institutions like the temple-banks of Uruk (circa 4000BC) functioned as the first artificial intelligences, evolving from simple grain storage into complex systems that controlled cities, armies, and societies. This perspective matters because it offers insights into how artificial goal-seeking systems can emerge and evolve beyond their original purpose, potentially informing modern AI alignment thinking.

**["These Parchment Barriers"](https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/these-parchment-barriers)** - Dean Ball introduces OpenAI's new AI Futures Blog, though details about the content and focus remain limited from the available description. This development is notable as it suggests OpenAI is expanding its public communication efforts around long-term AI impacts and policy considerations.

## Policy & Governance

**[How AI Could Hollow Out the U.S. Military](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/how-ai-could-hollow-out-the-u-s-military/)** - Georgetown CSET's Emelia Probasco examines how the Pentagon's growing integration of AI systems into military operations and training could undermine human decision-making capabilities and military judgment over time. This analysis is crucial because it highlights potential risks of over-reliance on AI in critical national security contexts where human judgment and adaptability remain essential.
