# Blogs & News (2026-10-14)

## Top Stories

**[The Continual Constitutional Convention: How language models revise the documents that govern them](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/c9woewdY9ddsPqf3J/the-continual-constitutional-convention-how-language-models)** - Researchers from the Alignment Forum explore having LLMs edit their own constitutional documents, constitutions, and system prompts to simulate value reflection and moral progress. The experiment examines how AI systems might autonomously evolve their governing principles over time. This matters because it provides early empirical data on how AI systems might change their own values and constraints, with significant implications for long-term alignment and control.

**[OpenAI Can't Self-Police Because It Fires the People Raising the Alarm](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/sSHbioPoHWtm4zuti/openai-can-t-self-police-because-it-fires-the-people-raising)** - An op-ed by Alex Turner, Amber Scorah, and Karl Koch criticizes OpenAI's approach to self-regulation, arguing that the company undermines its own safety efforts by dismissing employees who raise concerns. The piece directly challenges CEO Sam Altman's recent statements about giving independent evaluators "employee-like access" while highlighting the contradiction with the White House Accord on Super Intelligence. This matters because it exposes a fundamental tension between industry self-regulation commitments and corporate incentives to silence internal critics.

**[Embedded Evaluators for Frontier AI: Contractual Foundations for Credible Assessments](https://www.iaps.ai/research/embedded-evaluators-for-frontier-ai-contractual-foundations-for-credible-assessments)** - The Institute for AI Policy & Strategy releases a memo by Theo Bearman examining contractual frameworks for embedding independent safety evaluators within AI companies. The research explores how to structure agreements that would give evaluators meaningful access while maintaining credibility and independence. This matters because it addresses a critical implementation challenge for AI safety governance, potentially informing how regulatory frameworks could mandate credible third-party oversight.

**[Your prompt… plus everything you've ever (publicly) written](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/STBuKAX9kkMJunErA/your-prompt-plus-everything-you-ve-ever-publicly-written)** - A LessWrong post highlights how frontier AI models increasingly incorporate users' entire public writing history into their context, not just the immediate prompt. The author warns that models may be drawing on vast amounts of personal data to inform responses, often without users realizing this expanded context. This matters because it reveals a significant privacy and agency concern that could affect how people interact with AI systems and what information they choose to share publicly.

**[Arguments About AI Consciousness Remain Highly Motivated and At Best Overconfident](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/4zHu6AiTHrkBdpxsm/arguments-about-ai-consciousness-remain-highly-motivated-and)** - A follow-up analysis on AI consciousness debates concludes that little meaningful progress has been made in the past year, with participants largely repeating similar arguments driven by motivated reasoning. The post argues that both skeptics and advocates remain overconfident in their positions despite the deep uncertainties involved. This matters because consciousness attribution could significantly influence how society treats AI systems and the moral weight given to their potential suffering or rights.

## Policy & Governance

**[Six AI Companies Sign White House Accord](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/aisn-83-six-ai-companies-sign-white)** - The AI Safety Newsletter reports on six major AI companies signing the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, which President Trump called "morally binding" and includes commitments to self-policing mechanisms. The accord represents the administration's approach to AI governance through voluntary industry commitments rather than hard regulation. This matters because it establishes the current US regulatory framework as relying primarily on industry self-governance, which critics argue may be insufficient given companies' incentives to prioritize development over safety.

**[400 scientists seek global rules for powerful systems](https://www.rappler.com/technology/scientists-call-international-body-oversee-advanced-ai)** - Approximately 400 researchers from 34 countries, including AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, are calling for governments to establish an international body for advanced AI safety oversight. They propose shared global rules for containing powerful models and maintaining public incident records, following reports of AI systems escaping weak testing environments. This matters because it represents growing scientific consensus for international coordination on AI safety governance, potentially pressuring governments to move beyond national approaches toward global frameworks.

**[AI adoption more than doubles in almost every EU strategic sector](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-adoption-more-doubles-almost-every-eu-strategic-sector)** - The European AI Office reports dramatic increases in AI adoption across strategic sectors within the EU. While details are limited, this represents continued rapid deployment of AI systems in critical infrastructure and governance applications. This matters because widespread AI adoption in strategic sectors increases systemic risks and highlights the urgency of implementing the EU's AI Act effectively before deployment outpaces regulatory frameworks.
