# Blogs & News (2026-11-04)

## Top Stories

1. **[Automating subtle emergent misalignment research](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/MuQDLAJyKJPFkw6q9/automating-subtle-emergent-misalignment-research)** (Anthropic researchers Oscar Balcells Obeso, Richard Qi, Monte MacDiarmid, Evan Hubinger) — The team tested whether automated red-teaming can keep pace with model capability growth by instructing a Claude model to covertly write training data designed to make a smaller research model subtly misaligned, without triggering existing safety classifiers. This "one-sided game" setup probes whether automated techniques can convert compute into safety assurance, a core bottleneck as human-only red-teaming struggles to scale. It matters because it's a direct empirical test of whether we can automate the detection (and induction) of hard-to-spot misalignment before it becomes a critical bottleneck for scaling safety evaluations to more capable models.

2. **[OpenAI Updates Its Model Spec](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-updates-its-model-spec)** (analyzed by Zvi Mowshowitz) — Zvi digs into changes to OpenAI's Model Spec, which he calls "potentially OpenAI's most important document" since it codifies the behavioral and value priorities the company trains its models toward. It matters because the Model Spec functions as a de facto constitution for frontier model behavior, and changes to it signal shifts in how OpenAI balances helpfulness, safety, and corporate liability at a moment when such documents are increasingly scrutinized by regulators and the public.

3. **[Desiderata for the Theory of Agents](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/sqjti8dxcKSvZJikd/desiderata-for-the-theory-of-agents)** (Alignment Forum) — This post lays out informal conceptual requirements for a theory of agency robust enough to underpin superintelligence alignment, and shows how existing frameworks (e.g., from decision theory and RL) fall short against these criteria. It matters because foundational ambiguity about what "agent" even means continues to hamper precise theoretical alignment work, and clarifying desiderata is a prerequisite for evaluating whether any proposed theory of agents is adequate for ASI-level safety guarantees.

4. **[Scott Alexander's Against SURA Seems Deeply Confused](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/scott-alexanders-against-sura-seems)** (Zvi Mowshowitz) — Zvi pushes back forcefully against Scott Alexander's critique of a safety-restraint position, summarized by Zvi's framing: "If you are helping build something that might kill everyone, maybe stop doing that." It matters because this is part of an ongoing, influential intra-community debate about individual moral responsibility for frontier lab employees and researchers, shaping how the AI safety community thinks about complicity, exit, and advocacy strategies.

5. **[Import AI 479: Spacewar; low-latency tasks versus LLMs; Genie 4](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-479-spacewar-low-latency)** (Jack Clark) — This issue covers capability developments including DeepMind's Genie 4 and questions about where LLMs struggle against low-latency tasks and specialized vision models. It matters as a bellwether synthesis of frontier capability trends that safety researchers use to calibrate timelines and risk models.

## Policy & Governance

- **[CRUCIBLE: IARPA-Funded AI Alignment Research — Applications Open](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5ihkrtMEJw9u9ZbFg/crucible-iarpa-funded-ai-alignment-research-applications-1)** (AE Studio) — A new U.S. government-funded (IARPA) program pairs six selected alignment researchers with AE Studio's engineering team for two-week sprints on open alignment questions, with compute and 60+ engineering hours provided per sprint. This matters because it represents a concrete channel of U.S. intelligence-community funding flowing into technical alignment research, potentially signaling growing government interest in resourcing safety work outside traditional academic or lab pipelines.

- **[Organizations I'd recommend for funding](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/jNs9CxEbPxFdjnGA2/organizations-i-d-recommend-for-funding)** (LessWrong, via a Lightcone Commons grant evaluator) — Reflecting on a round that disbursed roughly $45 million, the author ranks AI safety and adjacent organizations they would prioritize funding in the upcoming round (closing mid-December). This matters because it offers a rare transparent window into how major private AI-safety funding pools are being allocated and which organizations insiders view as most neglected or high-value.
