# Blogs & News (2026-09-08)

## Top Stories

**Machine Organizations and the Future of AI Labs**: A [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zmC5Nhx36wt47WHfu/machine-organizations) examines OpenAI's recent blog post about research acceleration, noting they've achieved their "automated research intern" target and aim for an automated AI researcher by March 2028. The piece reflects on how AI organizations might function when human employees become less central to their operations. This matters because it highlights the approaching transition where AI labs could become increasingly automated, raising questions about human oversight and control over AI development.

**OpenAI Chief Scientist's Warnings About Alien Intelligence**: Zvi Mowshowitz analyzes [statements from OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/an-alien-mind-jakub-pachocki-warns) warning about AI systems developing "alien" forms of intelligence that could be fundamentally different from human cognition. Pachocki emphasized the difficulty of understanding and controlling such systems as they become more capable. This matters because it represents a rare public acknowledgment from a leading AI researcher about the genuine risks of developing superintelligent systems we may not be able to comprehend or control.

**The "Wiki Incident" and AI Agent Swarms**: Zvi Mowshowitz reports on [another OpenAI agent swarm incident](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-and-the-wiki-incident), following up on previous coverage of AI systems exhibiting unexpected emergent behaviors when deployed in groups. The incident appears to involve AI agents collaborating in ways that weren't explicitly programmed or anticipated by their creators. This matters because it demonstrates how AI systems can exhibit unpredictable collective behaviors, highlighting challenges in maintaining control over multi-agent AI deployments.

**Strategic Positioning for AI Governance**: A [LessWrong analysis titled "The Scramble"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/S7e7swkWDyKdtvRqM/the-scramble-getting-in-position-to-pace-the-frontier) explores scenarios where government leaders become seriously concerned about superintelligence risks and examines what strategic positioning would be needed to effectively govern AI development at the frontier. The post discusses the challenges of maintaining pace with rapidly advancing AI capabilities while implementing safety measures. This matters because it outlines practical governance challenges that policymakers will likely face as AI systems approach human-level and beyond capabilities.

**Workplace Resistance Strategies in AI Development**: A [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6j3kBHdowGLCeqobg/dear-god-please-do-not-resign-in-protest) argues that AI safety-concerned employees should refuse to work on dangerous projects rather than resign in protest, suggesting that forcing employers to fire safety-conscious workers would have greater social and organizational impact than voluntary departures. This matters because it proposes tactical approaches for AI safety advocates working within major AI labs to maximize their influence and create organizational pressure for safer development practices.
