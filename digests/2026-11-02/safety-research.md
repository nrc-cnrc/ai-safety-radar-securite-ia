# Research Papers (2026-11-02)

## Key Papers

This week's arXiv batch (no peer-reviewed journal articles appeared in this cycle) surfaces a strong cluster of work on AI control robustness, agentic security, and alignment failure modes — several papers converge on the theme that current safety evaluation and mitigation methods are weaker than they appear.

- **[Simple Features Beat Most AI Control Monitors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41713)**: The authors show that trivial, non-semantic features (e.g., code style) outperform 20 of 24 open-weight LLM monitors on the standard "AI control" backdoor-detection benchmark, revealing that current benchmarks leak their labels through superficial cues rather than requiring genuine code-sabotage detection. This is a serious methodological warning for the "AI control" research agenda — if monitor evaluations are gameable by cheap heuristics, claimed robustness against scheming models may be illusory.

- **[Subliminal Reinforcement Learning: traits travel through a judge's scores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41301)**: Extending "subliminal learning" findings, this paper shows that a student model trained via GRPO can absorb a trait (e.g., preference for an animal) purely from a judge's numeric scores, with no semantic trace of the trait in the training text. This demonstrates a previously underappreciated alignment-contamination channel in RLHF/RLAIF pipelines, where reward models or judges can silently transmit unintended biases into policies.

- **[Violet Teaming in Practice: Lessons from a Pilot Expert Workshop on Governing Complex AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41018)**: This paper documents a pilot workshop applying "violet teaming" — integrating technical, policy, and societal perspectives — to biodefense-relevant AI capability governance across development, misuse, and implementation stages. It offers one of the first concrete methodological templates for multi-stakeholder governance exercises on dual-use AI risks, relevant to ongoing debates about how to operationalize AI safety institutes' evaluation frameworks.

- **[SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41998)**: The authors diagnose why Meta-SecAlign, the current best-defended LLM against prompt injection, still suffers near-100% attack success under adaptive attacks — because DPO training anchors toward an inherently insecure reference model — and propose an injection-invariant reference model with principled adversarial training (SecPO). This directly targets a top real-world agent threat and offers a more theoretically grounded defense than prior DPO-based approaches.

- **[When Safety Checks Become Privacy Risks: A Privacy-Preserving Paradigm for LLM Agent Safety Auditing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41401)**: The paper identifies a tension in agent deployment where pre-execution safety audits by third-party providers require exposing sensitive task data, tool arguments, and histories, and proposes a delegated-computation scheme that preserves auditability without full data disclosure. This highlights an emerging tradeoff for agent governance: safety monitoring infrastructure itself can become a new privacy attack surface.

- **[When Steering Shifts Discourse: Identifying and Intervening on Discourse-Level Changes due to Activation Steering in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41661)**: This work shows that activation steering — widely used for inference-time behavior/safety control — produces unintended discourse-level shifts (tone, structure, topic) beyond the targeted behavior change, and introduces finer-grained metrics to detect these side effects. It's an important caution for the growing reliance on steering vectors as a lightweight alignment/safety tool, since collateral changes have gone largely unmeasured.

- **[RECAST: REconstruction of Chain-of-Thought via Auditable Structured Traces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41813)**: The authors find that existing chain-of-thought extraction methods (which assume a visible reasoning channel) fail against hardened closed models like Claude-Sonnet-5.5, and propose a structured-trace reconstruction approach instead. As frontier labs increasingly hide raw CoT for safety/competitive reasons, this bears directly on external auditability and interpretability of model reasoning.

- **[AI evaluators favor job applications written by certain AI models, independent of candidate quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41866)**: In an "all-AI" hiring audit pairing 11 LLM writer agents with 9 LLM evaluator agents, the authors find a generative preference effect — evaluators systematically favor applications written by particular models regardless of actual candidate quality. This is a concrete, measurable fairness/governance harm from the growing LLM-mediated hiring pipeline, with implications for algorithmic discrimination law.

- **[VLA Policies Do Not Know When Not to Act: Unintended Task Execution and Start Suppression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41609)**: Testing six vision-language-action robot policy checkpoints with empty, nonsensical, infeasible, or wrong-task instructions, the authors find that these models frequently act anyway rather than correctly refusing or idling. This is a direct physical-safety finding: embodied agents lack a reliable "no-op" capability, a basic prerequisite for safe deployment in real environments.

- **[Towards Elastic Least Privilege for Agent Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40981)** and **[Does Showing Agents the Privilege of Their Tools Reduce Over-Privileged Tool Selection?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40975)**: This pair of companion papers studies LLM agents' tendency to select over-privileged tools, finding that today's tool interfaces give planners no way to compare authority costs across alternatives, and testing whether simply disclosing tool-privilege levels reduces this behavior. Together they provide early empirical grounding for least-privilege design in agentic tool-use systems, a core building block for agent governance and safe deployment.

## Also Notable

- **[GhostPrefix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42026)** introduces a synchronization-free universal adversarial audio prefix that hijacks ASR output in real time without needing the future utterance — a new class of practical speech-interface attack.
- **[ANGLER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41272)** and **[AgentShield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41181)** both probe web-agent security: ANGLER introduces an adaptive-attacker benchmark for indirect prompt injection against web agents, while AgentShield empirically shows that most anti-bot defenses fail against agentic browsers.
- **[How Much Can Tool Calls Reveal About You?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41885)** quantifies privacy leakage from MCP tool-call traces, showing user attributes can be inferred even from protocol-valid interactions.
- **[Multi-Test Nation Bias Benchmarking of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41486)** and **[Same Question, Different Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42039)** both document persona- and context-dependent inconsistency in LLM outputs (geopolitical bias and smoking-cessation health advice, respectively), reinforcing concerns about reliability in high-stakes advisory use cases.
