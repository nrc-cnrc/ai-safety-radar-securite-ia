# Daily Digest (2026-11-12)

## Top 3 Developments

**CSET Warns Against Strategic Overreaction to Espionage Allegations Amid AI Infrastructure Backlash** - [Espionage, AI, and the Risks of Strategic Overreaction](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/espionage-ai-and-the-risks-of-strategic-overreaction/) examines the Nvidia-linked spying allegations (detailed in companion pieces on [policy contradictions](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/spy-scandal-highlights-contradictions-at-heart-of-trumps-china-policy/) and [the alleged spy's background](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/the-phantom-life-of-alice-huang-alleged-chinese-spy-who-had-affair-with-nvidia-ceo/)) and cautions that treating isolated incidents as coordinated PRC strategy risks damaging US compute infrastructure and leadership. This matters because how Washington calibrates export-control and data-center policy in response to espionage fears will materially shape the geography and pace of frontier AI development.

**AI Now Institute Surfaces Expert Rift Over Existential vs. Near-Term AI Harm Framing** - [The AI Safety Debate Has Lost the Plot](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/the-ai-safety-debate-has-lost-the-plot-we-asked-10-experts-what-the-real-nightmares-are), featuring Chief AI Scientist Heidy Khlaaf and nine other experts, argues public safety discourse has become overly fixated on speculative extinction scenarios at the expense of established risk-assessment frameworks for present-day harms. This matters because the divide between existential-risk and harms-focused camps directly affects how regulatory attention and resources get allocated going forward.

**Audit Finds a Widely-Used Cyber-Defense Benchmark Silently Miscounts Agent Actions** - [What a CAGE 4 Score Measures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14068) reproduces CAGE Challenge 4's reference multi-agent cyber-defenders in a pinned container and finds the simulator discards actions from busy agents and miscounts penalties, meaning leaderboard scores measure less than assumed. This matters as a cautionary case study showing that even cited safety/security benchmarks can harbor implementation bugs that distort comparative capability claims.
