# Blogs & News (2026-11-18)

## Top Stories

- **[Where is all the AI persuasion?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7FQMM394P4D7ZAsBj/where-is-all-the-ai-persuasion)** (LessWrong) — This post interrogates a core tension in AI risk discourse: a growing body of experimental research shows that AI systems are now more persuasive than expert humans, yet we see little evidence of this capability translating into real-world large-scale persuasion campaigns, election manipulation, or mass behavioral shifts. The piece pushes back on the assumption that "superhuman persuasion = societal takeover risk" by examining the gap between lab results and deployed impact. *This matters because persuasion is a foundational mechanism in several loss-of-control threat models — if the capability-to-impact gap is real and durable, it reshapes how urgently we should prioritize anti-persuasion defenses versus other alignment work.*

- **[Jensen Huang Really Liked Selling China Advanced AI Chips](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/jensen-huang-really-liked-selling)** (Zvi Mowshowitz) — Zvi frames newly surfaced details about NVIDIA's enthusiasm for selling advanced AI chips to China as "a really big deal," suggesting a scandal with significant implications for export control enforcement and the compute-governance regime that underpins much of current AI safety strategy (e.g., chip-based verification and monitoring proposals). *This matters because the credibility of hardware-based governance — a cornerstone of many proposed international AI safety frameworks — depends on export controls actually holding, and evidence of circumvention or lobbying against them undermines that foundation.*

- **[Fable 5.2 is the New Bazooka](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/fable-52-is-the-new-bazooka)** (Zvi Mowshowitz) — Zvi's characteristically terse framing ("You do not always want a bazooka") signals concern about a new model release (Fable 5.2) delivering capability gains that outpace the use cases or safeguards needed to wield it responsibly. *This matters as another data point in the recurring pattern of capability jumps outrunning deployment-readiness and misuse mitigation.*

- **[A wander through adult intelligence amplification](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ocywf8gX9mkNmBxdp/a-wander-through-adult-intelligence-amplification)** (LessWrong) — A dialogue-style exploration of biotechnological pathways to boosting adult human cognition, concluding there's currently no clear intervention with a solid causal case for raising general cognitive capacity. The post teases a forthcoming research agenda on the topic. *This matters to AI safety insofar as human intelligence amplification is sometimes proposed as a complementary (or alternative) strategy to AI alignment for keeping humans "in the loop" and competitive with advancing AI systems — and this piece suggests that pathway remains far less mature than AI capability progress itself.*
